01 / Why

28·04·202512:33 · Spain and Portugal

What if the internet goes down tomorrow?

That Monday, Spain and Portugal lost power for hours. Without electricity, cell towers lasted as long as their batteries did, and many phones were left with no data and no signal: no maps, no search, no way to look up what to do.

A blackout, a cyberattack, a storm or a massive IT failure is enough to silence your phone right when you need it most. Nomad Pocket keeps the essentials on the phone itself, so it keeps working when everything else fails.

Blackout

Without power, cell towers don’t last long. Your maps, guides and plan stay on your phone.

Internet outage

Your carrier or a big cloud service fails. Nothing you carry depends on them.

Cyberattack

If the networks or services you use are attacked, what’s yours stays on your phone, intact.

Storm or flood

While the network is up, official alerts tell you what’s coming; when the weather takes it down, you know where the hospital, the pharmacy or the water is.

No signal

Mountains, sea, a remote road: routes and answers without a single bar of signal.

Travelling

Abroad with no roaming or local SIM, with the local map and the emergency number of the country you’re in.

Maps and navigation

Maps that don’t switch off

Your whole country on your phone: every street, path and building. Get routes by car, on foot or by bike with no network, with spoken directions that keep guiding you with the screen locked and a new route if you leave it. With both countries downloaded, one route crosses the border.

  • Search streets, house numbers, places and categories with no internet, nearby first, with your recent places
  • Tap any point of the map: what it is, its address, its phone and whether it’s open now
  • By car, the road’s speed limit and a warning when you go over it
  • Compass, coordinates and UTM, saved places
  • Maps for 241 countries and territories, routes for 233, plus a built-in world map

CarPlay

Your route on your car’s screen

Connect your iPhone and Nomad Pocket shows up in CarPlay: the offline map and turn-by-turn navigation on your car’s screen, with spoken directions. Even with no signal, out on the open road.

  • Search for a destination, pick a saved place or the nearest hospital, pharmacy or fuel station
  • Maneuvers, distance and arrival time, your speed and the road’s speed limit sign
  • Start the trip on the phone or in the car: both stay in step

CarPlay: on iPhone.

Emergency

What you need, all around you

Hospitals and health centres, pharmacies, defibrillators, police, fire stations, drinking water, shelters, fuel stations, supermarkets and embassies: nearest first, with the distance and direction. One tap and it takes you there.

  • Call the emergency number of the country you’re in: it knows it offline
  • In 25+ countries, completed with official health registers
  • Share your exact position by message (coordinates and UTM)
  • GPS needs no data: you know where you are even with no signal

Official alerts

Warned before it hits

When AEMET, MeteoAlarm or another official service issues an orange or red warning for your area, or there’s a strong earthquake nearby, you get a notification as soon as it’s published, so you can get ready while there’s still a connection.

  • AEMET in Spain, MeteoAlarm in 35 European countries, the NWS in the US, GDACS elsewhere, and USGS and EMSC earthquakes
  • The official text, who issued it and until when, with the area on the map, the guide card, your plan and your bag
  • Optional: you choose from which level and whether you want earthquakes

Nomad Pocket doesn’t issue alerts. In an emergency, follow your authorities’ alerts and call the emergency number.

Emergency guide

What to do, step by step

200+ cards based on official sources: CPR, bleeding, burns, choking, fires, floods… In the app from the first minute, nothing to download.

  • Short texts, written to be read in a hurry
  • Every card names its source and license, and says whether it’s an official text or our wording
  • Instant search inside the guide

First aid and medical information: on iPhone and iPad. On Android, the rest of the guide.

Offline assistant

An AI that doesn’t need the internet

Ask “how do I make water safe to drink?” in the middle of nowhere: it answers from your library, the guide and your plan, and shows its sources. It runs on your phone, with an open model you download or, on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, Apple’s built-in model. On Android, with a model you download.

  • Ask it about the map: the nearest pharmacy, a route, where you are. The answer comes from your maps, not from the AI
  • Urgent questions get the guide’s card word for word, even with no model
  • Dictate your questions: your voice is processed on the device
  • Your questions never leave your phone

Health and first-aid answers: on iPhone and iPad.

Library

All the knowledge, no network

Wikipedia in Spanish or English, from its 50,000 best articles to the whole encyclopedia, to fit your storage. 80 archives on survival, water and food, farming, repairs and more, PDF guides, plus thousands from the Kiwix catalog.

  • Full-text search across all your books and their PDFs
  • 14 courses to learn whatever you need
  • Pass an archive to another phone with no internet
  • Read in dark or on a paper page

Plan and bag

Your family knows what to do

Meeting points on the map, contacts, important numbers and home notes, also as a printable PDF. And an emergency bag sized to your household, with expiry reminders.

  • Water, food and medication worked out for adults, children and pets, from 72 hours to two weeks
  • Local reminders before anything expires
  • Your plan never leaves your phone unless you share it (or in your backups)

03 / How it works

Ready in a minute

Set it up today, while you’re online. Tomorrow you won’t need to be.

  1. 01

    Pick your country

    The app proposes a kit sized for your country and your phone’s storage: map and routes, guides, Wikipedia and the assistant. And it asks whether you want official alerts.

  2. 02

    Download it on Wi‑Fi

    Leave it downloading with the app open. Home tells you how prepared you are and what’s missing, so you can finish while the network is up.

  3. 03

    Use it offline

    In airplane mode, in a blackout or in the middle of nowhere. And when your maps, archives or courses have newer versions, it lets you know and updates them without losing the old copy.

04 / Yours and nobody else’s

Private by design

Your plan, your contacts and your questions never leave your phone, and neither does your location unless you share it or turn on official alerts. Maps, guides, library and assistant depend on no server, so there’s nothing to go down. Only instant official alerts, if you turn them on, use a small server of ours that doesn’t know who you are: all it knows is the grid square of about 10 km you’re in.

05 / FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does it really work without internet?

Yes. You only need a connection to download the content (Wi‑Fi is best) and to check for updates. After that, maps, routes, street search, emergency points, the guide, the library and the assistant all work in airplane mode. Only official alerts need a connection to arrive.

Does GPS work without data?

Yes. GPS receives signals from satellites, not from the internet, so the map finds you even with no signal. The first fix may take a little longer than with data.

Does it warn me about storms or earthquakes?

Yes, if you turn on official alerts. It shows the warnings published by AEMET (Spain), the US National Weather Service, MeteoAlarm (35 European countries) and GDACS elsewhere, plus USGS and EMSC earthquakes, and notifies you of the orange and red ones for your area as soon as they’re published. Alerts need a connection to arrive. Nomad Pocket doesn’t issue alerts: in an emergency, follow your authorities’ alerts on your phone and call the emergency number.

How much space does it take?

You decide. The welcome wizard suggests a kit for your country and free space (map and navigation, essential guides, Wikipedia and the assistant) and you can switch off each piece. A country takes from under 1 MB (a small island) to about 11 GB (Germany); Spain is about 4.7 GB. The United States, Canada and Russia go by regions. The app shows the exact size before you download.

Does it work with CarPlay?

Yes. Connect your iPhone to the car and Nomad Pocket shows up in CarPlay with your country’s map: search for destinations, find emergency services nearby and get spoken directions, all offline. Not on Android Auto yet.

Does it work in my country?

There are maps for 241 countries and territories, and routes, street search and emergency points for 233; a world map is built in from the first second. The United States, Canada and Russia download by regions. In more than 25 countries, pharmacies, hospitals and health centres are completed with official registers. The app and its library are in English and Spanish (your own ZIM files can be in any language).

Is it free? What’s the catch?

No catch: it’s free, with no ads, no purchases and no subscriptions. It’s an open source project that doesn’t live off your data: there’s nothing to charge for and nothing to sell. If you’d like to support it, you can make a donation.

Which devices are supported?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 16.4 or later. Android 8.0 or later, coming soon to Google Play. The assistant uses an open model you download in the app (from 533 MB; larger ones need more memory) or, on iPhones and iPads that have it, Apple Intelligence.

What about Android?

It’s in a closed test on Google Play and opens to everyone once Google approves it, with maps, routes, emergency services, the library, the plan and the assistant. First aid and medical information are only on iPhone and iPad.

Do you collect my data?

No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking: what you do in the app stays on your device. The only thing we keep is for instant official alerts, if you turn them on: your phone’s notification identifier and the grid square of about 10 km you’re in (never your position), deleted when you turn them off. The details are in the privacy policy.

Does it replace the emergency services?

No. It helps when there’s no network, but the AI can make mistakes and medical information is no substitute for a professional. Official alerts don’t replace your authorities’ alerts on your phone. If you can, always call the emergency number.
