## Gaps and risks

1. **No current public-domain text for several vital paediatric and resuscitation protocols:** infant choking (1.4), child and infant CPR beyond one paragraph (1.2), drowning resuscitation (1.25), emergency childbirth (1.30, only a 1960s civil-defence booklet), and the response when a choking person goes unresponsive (ATP ¶5-36–5-39 is outdated: "rescue breathing" and "6 to 10 thrusts"). These are all current only in ERC, AHA or Red Cross material, which can't be copied. WHO material (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO) could only be an alternative with its licence, and its NC clause is a problem next to GPL distribution.
2. **The old Spanish-government first-aid texts are reusable but partly harmful.** INSST NTP 467 (choking: "never strike the back"), NTP 469 (arterial pressure points, tourniquet as a last resort, Trendelenburg) and NTP 246 (neutralise acids with bicarbonate) shouldn't be copied. NTP 1062 (2015 CPR) and NTP 524 (burns, 20–30 min cooling) are usable. The INTCF toxicology service's text is **not** reusable (Ministerio de Justicia notice). Only its phone number is.
3. **Many cards have no Spanish text from the same agency and would need a faithful translation.** That breaks the "no text of our own" rule unless the owner accepts translation as copying. Affected: 1.3, 1.5, 1.6, 1.8, 1.15, 1.17 (steps), 1.22, 1.24 (steps), 1.26, 2.4. ATP 4-02.11 is the main fallback and has no Spanish version.
4. **US-specific content in faithful copies:** 911 / 9-1-1, Poison Control 1-800-222-1222, CDC-INFO 800-232-4636, °F everywhere (95 °F, 100.4 °F, 59–86 °F), inches and pounds, US brand names (Tylenol, Advil, EpiPen, Humulin…), US snake species, and worker framing on NIOSH pages. ATP adds military wording (DD Form 1380, MEDEVAC, "Service member"). A faithful copy keeps all of this, so the app would need a visible "in Spain call 112" note outside the quoted text.
5. **Content and title risks:** card 2.3 asks a question that official sources answer "no" (FDA: don't use expired medicines). Card 2.2's "label doses" differ between the US (FDA, 4 g/day) and Spain (AEMPS prospecto, whose authorship and licence weren't verified). No public-domain source states burns "cool running water for 20 minutes" (ATP gives no duration) or gives a home ORS recipe. The CDC Spanish stroke page uses a different mnemonic from the English one. Several MedlinePlus Spanish summaries are years older than the English ones. HRSA Poison Help and the CDC "About Getting Clean" page couldn't be opened (403 and 404), so re-check them before relying on them.
