# Inventory 4: Outdoor survival cards (section 12) and licence check

Researched 2026-10-31. Rule applied: every card is a faithful copy of an official source we may legally
reproduce, with its reference. Only URLs that were actually opened are cited (pages that refused automated
access are listed at the end as "not verified").

## A. Section 12, Outdoor survival

### Base source: US Army ATP 3-50.21, *Survival* (Sept 2018), the current edition

- **It is current.** The Army Publishing Directorate record lists "ATP 3-50.21 · SURVIVAL · 09/18/2018 ·
  ACTIVE", with "Dist Restriction Code: A APPROVED FOR PUBLIC RELEASE; DISTRIBUTION IS UNLIMITED" and no
  superseding publication. Record: https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details_Printer.aspx?PUB_ID=1005316
  (also https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1005316).
- **Official PDF** (downloaded: 228 pages, 22.7 MB, letter size, created 19 Sep 2018):
  https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/ARN12086_ATP%203-50x21%20FINAL%20WEB%202.pdf
  Cover: "DISTRIBUTION RESTRICTION: Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited." No copyright,
  "courtesy of" or "reprinted with permission" notice appears anywhere in the text, so the figures are
  government work too.
- **Lineage.** Its introduction says the Personnel Recovery Proponent decided "to divide the revision of FM
  3-05.70, Survival from one large volume into separate ATPs". FM 3-05.70 (2002) superseded FM 21-76 (1992).
- **Avoid FM 3-05.70.** Its armypubs record now shows "C U.S. GOVERNMENT AGENCIES AND THEIR CONTRACTORS ONLY",
  footnote "PUB NOT RELEASABLE TO FOREIGNERS (NOFORN)", and "PRESCINDED BY PROP 06/12/2020":
  https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details_Printer.aspx?PUB_ID=78014. The text is still a US
  government work, but don't cite a publication whose current marking is restricted.
- **FM 21-76 (1992) and FM 21-76-1 / MCRP 3-02H (1999, multiservice "Survival, Evasion, and Recovery")** are
  only online as third-party mirrors (FAS, everyspec, archive.org, a NC state page). I opened no official copy,
  so they aren't used. ATP 3-50.21 covers the same ground except signaling.
- **USMC.** I found no public official copy of MCRP 3-02F (*Survival*, = FM 21-76). The USMC doctrine site
  needs a CAC login. Not used.
- **Signaling.** ATP 3-50.21 has no signaling section: no SOS, signal mirror or ground-to-air signals, only
  passing mentions of a signal fire. Card 12.6 therefore comes from the NPS (below).
- **Context to keep in mind.** The ATP is written for "isolated persons" awaiting "recovery forces", so pick
  paragraphs that read generically. Skip evasion, weapons, and survival medicine (chapter 2 is a health
  feature: HEALTH_FEATURES, hidden on Android).
- **No Spanish translation exists.** I found no official Spanish version of ATP 3-50.21, FM 3-05.70 or FM 21-76.
  Spanish-language "manual de supervivencia del ejército de EE. UU." editions are commercial translations,
  copyrighted by their translators, so we can't use them. A US federal work can be translated freely.

## B. Licence check

### B.1 US federal works (17 U.S.C. §105; CDC; FEMA/Ready.gov; NPS; NIH MedlinePlus)

**Allowed: yes**, with attribution, and with third-party items checked one by one.

- **§105.** "Copyright protection under this title is not available for any work of the United States
  Government". https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/105. This covers ATP 3-50.21, the CDC, FEMA,
  the NPS and NLM-authored pages.
- **CDC.** The material "is in the public domain" and "may be freely used or reproduced without obtaining
  copyright permission". Conditions:
  - "Attribution to the agency that developed the material must be provided".
  - Some items "are restricted in their use because they were developed by government contractors or
    grantees", and for non-PHIL images "the license holder may prohibit republication".
  - The logo "may not be used without express written permission".
  - Use "does not imply endorsement".
  - Source: https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html
  - Attribution: "Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), <page title>, <URL>, <date>."
- **FEMA / Ready.gov.** "Most material on FEMA.gov is free of copyright and may be copied and distributed
  without permission". Caveats:
  - "We sometimes use photos or graphics that we licensed or that are restricted. You need to request
    permission from the original source to use this material."
  - "Citation of FEMA.gov and a link back is much appreciated."
  - DHS seals and marks are governed by the DHS IP policy.
  - Source: https://www.fema.gov/about/website-information. Ready.gov's footer links its "Website Information"
    to this same FEMA page (https://www.ready.gov/), so the same terms apply.
  - Ready.gov's logo is a trademark: don't use it.
  - Attribution: "Source: FEMA / Ready.gov, <page>, <URL>."
- **NPS.** Content "unless otherwise indicated" is "generally considered in the public domain" and "may be
  distributed or copied as permitted by applicable law". Conditions:
  - "a citation or acknowledgement of the National Park Service as the source is appreciated".
  - Caveat: "Not all information or content on this website has been created or is owned by the National
    Park Service".
  - "The Arrowhead symbol may not be used without prior written permission".
  - Source: https://www.nps.gov/aboutus/disclaimer.htm
  - Attribution: "Source: U.S. National Park Service, Outdoor Emergency Plan, <URL>."
- **NIH MedlinePlus.** Public domain: works produced by the federal government, including "Summaries on
  health topic pages" and "Medical test information", in English and **Spanish**: "You may reproduce,
  redistribute, and link freely to non-copyrighted content".
  - **Licensed, do not copy:** "Any content from the A.D.A.M. Medical Encyclopedia", the ASHP "Drug
    monographs", and "Images, illustrations, and photos, except those noted above, may be copyright
    protected". Copyrighted items are labelled at the bottom of the page.
  - Required line: "Courtesy of MedlinePlus from the National Library of Medicine" (or "Source: MedlinePlus,
    National Library of Medicine").
  - Source: https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/
  - The page doesn't mention Natural Medicines (herbs and supplements). Treat those as licensed.
  - MedlinePlus *en español* health-topic summaries are an official Spanish text we can copy as-is (useful
    for the health cards).
- **Caveat for all US works.** §105 is US law. Outside the US the agencies' own "free to reproduce" statements
  above are what we rely on. That's low risk, but it's another reason to keep the attribution.

### B.2 Spain: Ley 37/2007 and the sites' own notices

**Allowed: yes for the state sites below**, including commercial use, under the general conditions. The
Guardia Civil is the exception (see the last bullet).

- **Ley 37/2007.** Consolidated text: https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-19814
  - Art. 4.1 sets the modes of reuse, including "Reutilización de documentos puestos a disposición del
    público sin sujeción a condiciones" and reuse under "licencias-tipo".
  - Art. 8 lists the general conditions that may apply: "Que el contenido de la información, incluyendo sus
    metadatos, no sea alterado", "Que no se desnaturalice el sentido de la información", "Que se cite la
    fuente", "Que se mencione la fecha de la última actualización".
  - Art. 4.9 forbids implying that the public body sponsors the reuse.
  - The law excludes "Los documentos sobre los que existan derechos de propiedad intelectual o industrial
    por parte de terceros".
  - The law applies to all administrations, so regional 112 services too, but each site's own notice decides
    the details.
- **Protección Civil** (https://www.proteccioncivil.es/aviso-legal)
  - The general notice says: "Se prohíbe la reproducción total o parcial de los contenidos de esta Web sin
    citar su origen o solicitar autorización". So reproduction **with the source cited is fine**.
  - The reuse section grants reuse for "fines comerciales y no comerciales" with "cesión gratuita y no
    exclusiva de los derechos de propiedad intelectual", and "documento" includes "forma de expresión
    gráfica, sonora o en imagen", so the infographics are covered.
  - Conditions: "Está prohibido desnaturalizar el sentido de la información"; give the last-update date if
    the original has one; don't suggest sponsorship; keep the metadata.
  - Attribution formula: **"Origen de los datos: Dirección General de Protección Civil y Emergencias."**
  - Don't reproduce the emblem as our branding.
- **Ministerio del Interior** (https://www.interior.gob.es/opencms/es/aviso-legal/): same model
  ("Aviso legal para la modalidad general…", RD 1495/2011, datos.gob.es/avisolegal). Reuse "para fines
  comerciales y no comerciales" includes "copia, difusión, modificación, adaptación". Attribution "Origen de
  los datos: Ministerio del Interior".
- **Ministerio de Sanidad** (https://www.sanidad.gob.es/avisoLegal/home.htm)
  - The information "es susceptible de reutilización", with "su reproducción total o parcial, modificación,
    distribución y comunicación" authorised "para usos comerciales y no comerciales".
  - Conditions: no "desnaturalización"; "citar la fuente"; "mencionar la fecha de la última actualización".
  - Logos, trademarks and "elementos gráficos asociados a campañas institucionales" are protected: copy the
    text, not campaign artwork.
- **AESAN** (https://www.aesan.gob.es/aviso-legal). The same wording, verbatim: "salvo indicación expresa en
  contrario, es susceptible de reutilización; quedando autorizada su reproducción total o parcial,
  modificación, distribución y comunicación, para usos comerciales y no comerciales". Conditions: no
  desnaturalización, cite the source, give the last-update date. Logos and campaign graphics are protected.
- **Ministerio de Defensa / Ejército de Tierra** (https://ejercito.defensa.gob.es/aviso-legal.html)
  - The RD 1495/2011 general reuse terms apply ("Origen de los datos: Ministerio de Defensa").
  - The page also says "La presente publicación … esta bajo licencia: Reconocimiento-CompartirIgual CC BY-SA"
    (no version given).
  - It notes the law excludes "documentos que afecten a la defensa nacional".
  - Images are stated to be protected.
  - I found no public Ejército de Tierra survival manual.
- **112 services (regional, examples)**
  - La Rioja: "Se autoriza la reproducción siempre que se haga referencia a la fuente, mientras no se indique
    lo contrario" (https://web.larioja.org/aviso-legal, which covers larioja.org/emergencias-112).
  - Comunidad de Madrid (transparency portal): "La Comunidad de Madrid permite la reutilización de todos los
    contenidos y datos difundidos por medio de este sitio", citing the source and date. It excludes content
    "sujetos a propiedad intelectual o industrial". The notice covers that portal; it doesn't name
    Madrid 112/ASEM112 (https://www.comunidad.madrid/transparencia/aviso-legal).
  - Check each 112 site's notice before using it.
- **Guardia Civil: do not use without written permission.** Its notice
  (https://web.guardiacivil.es/es/informacion-general/nota-legal/index.html, apparently its intranet
  template) limits the licence to "la descarga … y al uso privado" and says reproduction or distribution
  "queda totalmente prohibida salvo que medie autorización expresa de la Dirección General de la Guardia
  Civil". This is the only Spanish source checked that forbids our use.

### B.3 EU: Commission reuse policy (Decision 2011/833/EU, CC BY 4.0)

**Allowed: yes.**

- **Decision 2011/833/EU** (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011D0833)
  - Art. 4: "All documents shall be available for reuse: (a) for commercial or non-commercial purposes".
  - Art. 6(2) allows conditions such as "the obligation for the reuser to acknowledge the source" and "not
    to distort the original meaning or message".
  - Art. 2(2) excludes "logos and names" and "documents for which the Commission is not in a position to
    allow their reuse in view of intellectual property rights of third parties".
- **Legal notice** (https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en). Commission-owned content "is licensed
  under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence", reuse is allowed "provided
  appropriate credit is given and changes are indicated", and third-party works and logos are excluded.
- **CC BY 4.0 inside a GPL app.** It works as separately licensed content. A translation counts as a change,
  so mark it.
- **Attribution:** "© European Union, <year>. Source: European Commission, <title>, <URL>. Licence CC BY 4.0.
  Translated/excerpted by Nomad Pocket."
- **Not checked:** I didn't open the 2025 Preparedness Union Strategy page itself. Confirm that the 72-hour
  text is Commission-owned (not a member-state or third-party graphic) when that card is sourced.

### B.4 WHO (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO)

**Allowed only with friction: avoid, as the owner prefers.**

- Source: https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright
- WHO allows copying, translating and adapting "for non-commercial purposes", but "Permission is required
  for commercial uses".
- Translations and adaptations need WHO's disclaimer and "the same or a similar licence".
- "WHO logo cannot be used without written authorization", and the work can't be used "to promote or
  endorse products, services or any specific organization".
- **Why it doesn't fit a GPL app:**
  - NC conflicts with the GPL's freedom to sell copies, so WHO text can't be relicensed under the GPL.
  - It could only ship as a separately licensed content file, and that would bind every fork or
    redistributor to non-commercial terms.
  - SA would force our Spanish translations to be CC BY-NC-SA.
  - Use WHO only where no US, EU or Spanish source exists, and then as a clearly separate NC-SA asset.

### B.5 US Army publications ("distribution is unlimited")

**Allowed: yes.**

- Distribution Statement A, per DoDI 5230.24 as reproduced by DTIC's marking guide: "DISTRIBUTION STATEMENT A.
  Approved for public release: distribution is unlimited". In its category table, "PUBLIC RELEASE" maps only
  to A. All other statements (B–F) restrict who may receive the document.
  - https://discover.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/GuideToMarkingDocuments_2023.pdf
  - Same wording in the Sept 2018 chart: https://discover.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/distribution_statements_and_reasonsSept2018.pdf
- So an A-marked manual has been cleared for the public with no limit on further dissemination. Combined with
  §105, it can be copied, translated and redistributed, including outside the US.
- What A doesn't cover: Army or DoD insignia, and any implication of endorsement. Don't use the Army star or
  TRADOC logos.
- The "DESTRUCTION NOTICE" on the ATP cover is generic boilerplate and doesn't restrict a public-release
  document.
- Check the *current* marking on armypubs. FM 3-05.70 is now distribution C/NOFORN even though older copies
  circulate freely.
- **Attribution:** "Source: Headquarters, Department of the Army, ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (September 2018),
  ¶<x-y>. Approved for public release; distribution is unlimited. Unofficial Spanish translation by Nomad
  Pocket."
