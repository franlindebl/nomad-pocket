# Source inventory 2: Water, Food, Power, Cold/Heat, Hygiene (sections 3, 4, 5, 6, 11)

Checked 2026-10-31. Every URL below was opened during this check. A few sites refused automated fetches (HTTP 403):
fsis.usda.gov, foodsafety.gov, fcc.gov, and one interior.gob.es PDF. Their pages are **not** listed and have **not** been verified.
"Date" is the date the page shows: CDC shows one date (reviewed or updated); Ready.gov shows "Last updated" in mm/dd/yyyy.

## Licence basis (applies to every row)

- **US federal (CDC, EPA, FEMA/Ready.gov, FDA, CPSC, USFA):** these are US government works (17 USC 105), so they are public domain.
  CDC's reuse page (https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html, reviewed 2026-10-28) sets four conditions:
  credit the agency ("Source: CDC"), show a prominent non-endorsement disclaimer, make **no substantive changes**, and state
  that the material is available free on the agency's website. The page doesn't mention translations. Logos and third-party
  or stock images are excluded. FEMA/Ready.gov follow the same model: reproduce unaltered and don't imply endorsement.
- **Spanish state bodies:** all allow commercial and non-commercial reuse if you cite the source, don't distort the meaning,
  and state the date of last update.
  - Sanidad (https://www.sanidad.gob.es/avisoLegal/home.htm) and AESAN (https://www.aesan.gob.es/aviso-legal): logos and
    campaign graphics are excluded.
  - La Moncloa (https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/Paginas/es_AvisoLegal.aspx): credit line "Origen de los datos: sitio web de
    lamoncloa.gob.es. Ministerio de la Presidencia".
  - Protección Civil (https://www.proteccioncivil.es/aviso-legal): credit line "Origen de los datos: Dirección General de
    Protección Civil y Emergencias". It also forbids implying endorsement and requires keeping the update-date metadata.
- **Regional sites (Comunidad de Madrid etc.):** their own terms, which I did not check.
- **FEMA 477 / ARC A5055 "Food and Water in an Emergency" (Aug 2004):** co-branded with the American Red Cross, so the
  licence is not clean. It is also outdated (see 3.2). Don't use it.

## Gaps and risks

1. **Bleach doses don't map to Spanish lejía.** CDC's single table is for 5–9 % bleach; EPA's is for 6 %/8.25 %. Spanish
   "lejía" is legally 35–60 g/l active chlorine (≈3.5–6 %, RD 349/1993), and the label of "apta para agua de bebida" lejía
   must state the dose. I found no Spanish-government emergency drops-per-litre text.
   - A faithful CDC/EPA copy shown in Spain gives a dose that may be too low for a 3.5–4 % bottle.
   - The copy can't add "follow your label" unless a source says it.
   - Older US texts are still live and double the dose (Ready.gov ES "16 gotas por galón", FEMA 2004). Don't copy them.
   - Container cleaning (1 tsp/quart) and surfaces (5 tbsp/gal) also assume 5–9 %.
2. **US sources contradict each other.**
   - Altitude for the 3-min boil: CDC 6,500 ft, EPA 5,000 ft / 1,000 m.
   - Toilet-tank and rain water: CDC allows them; FEMA says avoid.
   - Can sanitising: Ready.gov 1:10, CDC 1 cup/5 gal, Sanidad boil then dry.
   - Fridge threshold: 40 °F in US sources, 5 °C in Spain.
   - Each card must follow one source and say which.
3. **Spanish versions lag or are missing.**
   - Older than the English page: CDC ES food-after-disaster (May 2024 vs Nov 2025), CDC ES diarrhoea PDF (2017), CDC ES
     infant FAQ PDF (2022 vs 2026), CDC ES handwashing PDF (2017), Ready.gov ES heat (Apr vs Jul 2026), FDA ES devices
     (2018, a different page).
   - No Spanish version at all for 4.4 home canning, 4.7 mushrooms, 11.2 masks, 11.3 caring for the sick, 11.4 general
     bleach. These need a faithful translation, or the Spanish-government text instead.
   - AESAN's canning PDF is image-only and would have to be transcribed.
4. **Cards without a clean source.**
   - 11.5 bucket toilet / latrine: there is no current federal household text; the CDC page is archived and aimed at
     responders.
   - 3.5 draining pipes and the water heater: only FEMA 477 (2004, co-branded with Red Cross, outdated) has the steps.
   - 4.7 wild plants: nothing found; mushrooms is two MMWR sentences.
   - Spanish heating habits (braseros, butane stoves): no source found. A DGPCE "fenómenos meteorológicos adversos" guide
     exists on interior.gob.es but returned 403, so it is not verified.
   - Spanish cold-weather advice: no Spanish state page opened.
   - FSIS, FoodSafety.gov and FCC returned 403, so they weren't checked.
5. **US-specific content and health gating.**
   - Faithful copies carry gallons, quarts, teaspoons, feet and °F. CDC ES pages are mostly imperial; Ready.gov ES often
     adds metres and °C.
   - They also carry 911, 9-1-1, 2-1-1, Poison Control 1-800-222-1222, NWS warning terms, the FEMA app and SMS codes,
     "FDA-approved" containers, the USDA canning guide, and "notify your electric company".
   - CDC requires no substantive changes, so these can't be swapped for 112. They can only be excerpted out, with an
     editor's note kept outside the quoted text.
   - Several cards here contain health content that the Android HEALTH_FEATURES gate may need to cover: 4.6 breastfeeding,
     5.5 medical devices, 6.1 and 6.2 hypothermia and heat-stroke signs, 11.2 masks, 11.3 warning signs, 4.4 botulism
     symptoms.
   - Every CDC card also needs the CDC attribution, the non-endorsement line and "available free at cdc.gov". Spanish
     sources need the source and the date of last update.
