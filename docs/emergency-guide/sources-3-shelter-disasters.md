# Source inventory 3: shelter, communication, disasters, CBRN, coping (cards 7, 8, 9, 10, 13)

Checked 2026-10-31. Every URL below was opened in this session; dates are as the page shows them
(Ready.gov writes MM/DD/YYYY). Word counts are approximate and count only the main text.

**Licence keys**

- **US-PD**: US federal government work (17 U.S.C. §105). FEMA "Website Information" (updated 1 May 2023,
  https://www.fema.gov/about/website-information): "Most material on FEMA.gov is free of copyright and may be copied and
  distributed without permission", but licensed or restricted photos and graphics need permission from their owner. Ready.gov
  terms (https://www.ready.gov/terms-and-conditions, 02/07/2013): "The Ready workmark logo and related marks are trademarked".
  So copy the text and leave out the logos and photos. CDC, USFA, CISA and NWS follow the same rule.
- **ES-PC**: proteccioncivil.es Aviso legal (https://www.proteccioncivil.es/aviso-legal), run by the DGPCyE. It authorises
  reuse "para fines comerciales y no comerciales", including copying, distribution and transformation, as long as you cite the
  source, don't distort the meaning, show the last-update date when the original has one, don't imply endorsement and keep the
  metadata. These are Ley 37/2007 (RISP) terms.
- **ES-IGN**: IGN Aviso legal (https://www.ign.es/web/ign/portal/info-aviso-legal). It opens with a general ban unless the IGN
  gives written permission, then allows "uso, reproducción total o parcial, distribución, comunicación pública, reutilización"
  of texts and documents under the RISP conditions in section 5, with the source cited. The wording is ambiguous, so it's worth
  emailing sismologia@mitma.es to confirm.
- **NIMH-PD**: the page says itself: "La información en esta publicación es de dominio público y se puede reutilizar y copiar
  sin necesidad de pedir permiso". Images are excluded, and NIMH should be cited.

**Important format note on Protección Civil.** The national recommendations on proteccioncivil.es are **infographics only**:
SVG files with their text converted to outlines, so there's nothing to extract. A faithful copy means transcribing the image
text word for word, or showing the image. None of them carries a date (only "© 2026"), and none has an English version.

## Gaps and risks

1. **Spanish Protección Civil can't be used as-is for earthquakes.** Its indoor earthquake infographic says to shelter
   "debajo de dinteles de las puertas", which contradicts Drop–Cover–Hold On and "avoid doorways" (Ready.gov, and IGN's own
   2020 poster). Use IGN or Ready.gov/es for 9.1. The PC chemical infographic also says to tape windows "en forma de cruz", a
   contested measure; review before copying it into 7.3 or 10.3.
2. **PC content is images without dates or English.** The text is outlined in SVG, so it has to be transcribed from the image
   or the image shown; there is no update date to display; and every PC card needs a faithful EN translation. Pairing PC (ES)
   with Ready.gov (EN) gives two different texts. Decide per card: one US agency in both languages (Ready, CDC, USFA, CISA), or
   Spain-specific PC/IGN text plus a translation. PC/IGN are the only ones that say **112**.
3. **No usable source yet** for 8.4 distress signals: ATP 3-50.21 has no signalling chapter, FM 3-05.70 is inactive and
   wasn't retrieved, and fcc.gov and irp.fas.org were blocked. 10.6 suspicious object and 8.3 ES-Alert (EN side) need
