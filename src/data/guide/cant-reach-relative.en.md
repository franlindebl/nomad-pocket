In a blackout or a big emergency, the most likely reason is that their phone has run out of battery or the network isn't working. It doesn't mean something has happened.

1. **Send them a message** (SMS or WhatsApp) and wait: when the network is overloaded, messages get through better than calls. Don't call again and again: you overload the network and drain your battery.
2. **Try other ways**: their landline, if they have one, or the mobile of someone who lives with them or nearby.
3. **Try again from time to time**, without running down your battery.
4. **Ask someone to go and check**: a neighbour, the building's caretaker, a relative or a friend who lives nearby.
5. **Go in person if it's safe to get there**: without crossing flooded areas or closed roads, and following what the authorities say.
6. **Call 112** if they are older or dependent, live alone, depend on oxygen or another electrical device, or you fear they have fallen or are ill, and nobody can go and check. Give their address and how to get into the home. Don't call just to ask whether their area has coverage or power.

## If you still have no news

- To report a missing person **you don't have to wait 24 hours**: go to the National Police or the Guardia Civil as soon as possible. First, look in their home and in the last place they were seen: they may have fallen or be injured.
- In large disasters, or if your relative is in a country at war or hit by a disaster, the Spanish Red Cross's Restoring Family Links service helps to find separated relatives (900 221 122, busquedas@cruzroja.es).
