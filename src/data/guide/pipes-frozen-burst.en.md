## If the pipe is frozen (no water comes out)

1. **Open the tap** on that pipe. Water will flow as soon as it thaws and the ice will not build up pressure.
2. **Warm it slowly**, starting at the tap end and working towards the frozen part: a hairdryer on a low setting, or towels soaked in hot water.
3. **Never use a flame** (blowtorch, lighter, gas hob) or a combustion heater near the pipe.
4. If you see a crack or a bulge, or it leaks as it thaws, **close the stopcock** and treat it as a burst pipe.
5. If you cannot thaw it, call a plumber.

## If the pipe has burst

1. **Close the stopcock** for the home (or for that section, if it has one). This comes first.
2. **If water reaches sockets, the fuse box or the electrical panel**, do not step in the water or touch anything electrical. If the floor around the panel is wet, do not go near it: call 112 or the electricity company and keep everyone away from the water. Switch off the main switch only from a clearly dry place.
3. **Open the taps** to drain the water left in the pipe.
4. **Contain the water:** towels, rags and buckets. Move furniture, appliances and documents away from the wet area.
5. **Temporary fix:** wrap the crack with a cloth and tie it or bind it with tape. It is only to get by until the plumber arrives.
6. **Take photos** of the damage before cleaning up, tell your insurer and call a plumber.

## So it doesn't happen again

- On freezing nights, leave the tap running in a thin trickle.
- Open the cupboard doors under the sink so the warm air of the house reaches the pipes.
- Insulate pipes that run outside or through unheated places.
- Disconnect outdoor hoses and drain those pipes.
- Learn today where your stopcock is.
