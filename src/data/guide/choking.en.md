To assess and manage airway obstruction, start by asking the casualty if they can speak or if they are choking.

[…]

If the casualty can speak or cough effectively, encourage them to continue coughing. This indicates that they still have good air exchange, and you should not interfere with their efforts to expel the obstruction.

However, if you notice high-pitched sounds when the casualty breathes or coughs, indicating poor air exchange, or if the casualty is unable to breathe, immediate action is required. Call for help and prepare to perform manual thrusts to clear the obstruction. Use abdominal thrusts, also known as the Heimlich maneuver, by positioning your hands between the waist and the rib cage and delivering quick upward thrusts. For individuals in advanced stages of pregnancy, those who are markedly obese, or if there is a significant abdominal wound, use chest thrust instead. This involves placing your hands in the center of the breastbone and pushing sharply inward and upward.

[…]

### Apply Abdominal Thrusts

Prior to applying abdominal thrust, first responders should administer five back blows. Abdominal thrust can be accomplished by using the following procedures:

- Stand behind the casualty and wrap your arms around their waist.
- Make a fist with one hand and grasp it with the other. The thumb side of your fist should be against the casualty’s abdomen, in the midline and slightly above the casualty’s navel, but well below the tip of the breastbone (figure 5-12 on page 68).
- Press the fists into the abdomen with a quick backward and upward thrust (figure 5-13 on page 68).
- Each thrust should be a separate and distinct movement.
- Continue performing abdominal thrusts until the obstruction is expelled or the casualty becomes unresponsive.

### Apply Chest Thrusts

An alternate technique to the abdominal thrust is the chest thrust. This technique is useful when the casualty has an abdominal wound, is pregnant, or when the casualty is so large that you cannot wrap your arms around the abdomen. To apply chest thrusts with casualty sitting or standing:

- Stand behind the casualty and wrap your arms around their chest with your arms under their armpits.
- Make a fist with one hand and place the thumb side of the fist in the middle of the breastbone (take care to avoid the tip of the breastbone and the margins of the ribs).
- Grasp the fist with the other hand and exert thrusts (figure 5-14 on page 69).
- Each thrust should be delivered slowly, distinctly, and with the intent of relieving the obstruction.
- Perform chest thrusts until the obstruction is expelled or the casualty becomes unresponsive.
