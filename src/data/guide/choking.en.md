For adults and children over 1 year. Suspect choking if the person suddenly can't speak, cough or breathe, especially while eating. Ask them: "Are you choking?"

1. **If they can cough**, encourage them to keep coughing and watch them.
2. **Get help.** Have someone call the emergency number, with the speaker on.

## If they can't cough or the cough stops working

3. **Up to 5 back blows.** Stand behind them and lean them forward, supporting their chest with one hand. With the heel of your other hand, give sharp blows between the shoulder blades.
4. **Up to 5 abdominal thrusts.** If they are still choking, stand behind them and put your arms around the upper part of their abdomen. Lean them forward. Make a fist and place it between the navel and the bottom of the breastbone. Grasp it with your other hand and pull sharply inwards and upwards.
5. **Alternate** 5 blows and 5 thrusts until the object comes out or they stop responding. If nobody has called the emergency number yet, call now.
6. **Stop** as soon as they cough, breathe noisily or cry.
7. **Late pregnancy, or if you can't get your arms around the abdomen:** give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. Put your arms around the chest under the armpits, place your fist in the middle of the breastbone, grasp it with your other hand and press sharply inwards. Alternate 5 blows and 5 chest thrusts.
8. Don't put a finger blindly into their mouth. Remove the object only if you can see it clearly.

## If they stop responding

9. Lay them face up on the floor. Call the emergency number if nobody has.
10. **Start CPR** straight away. For a child, start with 5 rescue breaths. Chest compressions can dislodge the object: carry on until help arrives even if it doesn't come out.

## Afterwards

11. If you gave abdominal or chest thrusts, have a healthcare professional check them even if they now feel fine: the thrusts can cause injuries.
