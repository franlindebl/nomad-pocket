## If you can't get dialysis

1. **Call your dialysis unit** to find out where and when to get dialysis. If they don't answer, call the back-up centre. If you can't reach anyone, go to the nearest hospital emergency department and say you need dialysis.
2. Try to get dialysis no later than **3 days** after your last session.
3. **Start the emergency diet now**, as soon as you might miss a session. Starting early does no harm. It does not replace dialysis.

## Emergency diet

- **Fluids:** half of what you normally drink. Milk, soup, juice and the liquid in tins count (drain them). Take tablets with apple sauce or very little water. For thirst: hard sweets, chewing gum, rinsing your mouth.
- **Nothing high in potassium:** bananas, avocado, kiwi, dried fruit (raisins, dried apricots), potatoes, tomatoes, beans and lentils, vegetable juices, sports drinks.
- **No salt**, salty foods or salt substitutes (they contain potassium).
- **Protein:** half your usual meat, fish and eggs, and very little milk. No drinks with phosphates.
- **Energy:** white bread, rice, pasta, unsalted crackers, oil and, without diabetes, sugar or honey. With diabetes, avoid sweets but keep sugar at hand for a low.

**Call 112** if you are very short of breath or have chest pain, palpitations, an irregular pulse or great weakness. Say you are on dialysis.

## Peritoneal dialysis at home without power

1. The cycler does not work without power. Do **manual exchanges** only if you have been trained and have manual supplies that are not out of date. If not, call your unit and start the emergency diet.
2. **Hands:** wash them with soap and bottled or boiled water, dry them with clean paper and use hand gel if you have it. Follow the technique you were taught step by step. Don't use doubtful water for any of the supplies.
3. **Bag:** warm it only with dry heat until it is lukewarm, never hot, and never in water. If you can't warm it, use it at room temperature and let it run in slowly, to avoid cramps.
4. **Tell your unit at once** if the fluid comes out cloudy or you have belly pain, fever or vomiting: it may be peritonitis.
