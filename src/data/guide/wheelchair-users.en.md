## Fire and you can't use the stairs

1. **Don't use the lift.**
2. **Call 112 now.** Give the address, floor and flat, say you use a wheelchair and can't get down the stairs.
3. **If the fire is in your home**, get out and close the door. Wait somewhere without smoke, by the stairwell door or in a neighbour's flat.
4. **If you can't leave your home**, close the door, seal the gaps with cloth or tape and go to a room with a window onto the street.
5. **Signal** from the window with a light-coloured cloth or a torch. Ask neighbours going down to tell the firefighters where you are.

## If you help someone down

- **Don't do it alone** if it can wait: take them somewhere without smoke, stay with them and have someone tell the firefighters where you both are.
- Ask them how they want to be helped. Don't carry them over your shoulder or press on their chest.
- If there is an evacuation chair, use it. A power chair is not taken down with the person in it.
- Manual chair and no other way out: stand behind, one step higher, hold the handles, tilt the chair back until it balances and lower the big wheels one step at a time. Better with a second helper in front, lower down, holding the frame without lifting it.

## Power chair without battery

- Use it only for what is essential.
- Charge it from a generator, always outdoors, or in the car, in the open air, with a converter plugged into the cigarette lighter. Ask the town hall where there is a place with power.
- If it runs flat: switch it off and, on level ground, move the freewheel levers on the motors (see the manual). It can then be pushed, but it has no brakes.

## Flat tyre and water close by

1. Don't stop to fix it: move away from the water now, to higher ground or an upper floor.
2. Ask anyone nearby to push you and call 112: say where you are and that you can't move on your own.
3. Never cross moving water: 15 cm is enough to knock a person down.
