A baby is safer a little cool than too hot: keep them warm, but don't overdo it.

1. **For sleep, a baby sleeping bag** or an extra layer of clothing is better than loose blankets. Dress them in at most one layer more than you wear to be comfortable in that room. If you use blankets, keep them thin, tucked in and no higher than their shoulders, with their feet touching the foot of the cot. A blanket folded over counts as two.
2. **No hat indoors.** Head and face always uncovered while asleep.
3. **Never** hot-water bottles or an electric blanket. The cot away from radiators, heaters and fires. A portable heater never pointed at the baby. Turn off gas and electric heaters at night.
4. **Feel their chest or the back of their neck**, not their hands or feet: those are usually cold, and that's normal. If they are sweaty or damp, take off a layer. If their chest is cold, add a thin layer.
5. **Always on their back**, on a firm, flat mattress, with no pillows, duvets, cushions or soft toys. Safest is their own cot in your room, next to your bed.
6. **If they sleep in your bed**, don't put them under your duvet: keep your bedding at your waist height, away from their face, and wear more clothes yourself. Your body warms them too. Don't share the bed if anyone in it smokes, has drunk alcohol or taken drugs or medicines that make them drowsy, if you are exhausted, or if the baby is under 4 months, was born premature or had a low birth weight.
7. **Never fall asleep with the baby on a sofa or an armchair.**

## If the baby is cold

8. Hold them skin to skin on your chest, both covered with a dry blanket. If you feel sleepy, first put them down on their back in their cot.
9. **Call 112 or go to the emergency department** if they are cold to the touch and also floppy, very quiet or drowsy, won't feed, or have cold, bright red skin.
