## What should I do if I receive an alert?

If one is received, first check whether it is a drill or a test of the system, which will be clearly stated in the message (although tests will be announced in advance through different media, you may not have received these prior notices). In that case, obviously, there is no need to do anything. Otherwise you should:

- Stay calm and read the text of the alert carefully, including the instructions on how to act.
- Tell the people around you. Most of them will have received the alert too, but some may not have.
- Follow the instructions included in the message.
- Stay attentive to other communication channels (radio, TV, social media…).

## How will the alerts reach me?

The alert will be shown on the mobile phone in a similar way to a text message, taking up the full width of the screen, and it will always come with a beep and a vibration.

Given the urgency of the situations for which alerts will be issued, the beep and the vibration will happen even if the phone is silenced, locked, or in “Do not disturb” mode.

## Can I turn off receiving the alerts?

Because of the seriousness and urgency of the situations the system will be used for, the settings defined for the devices do not allow the “Civil Protection Alerts”, which correspond to the highest alert level (Level 1) of the EU-Alert protocol, to be turned off.

The “Civil Protection Pre-Alerts”, corresponding to Level 2 of EU-Alert, because they are an advance warning, can be turned off on the device.

## What happens if an alert is sent to the area where I am and my phone is:

- Switched off? Phones that are switched off will NOT receive or show the alerts. If a phone is switched on in an affected area while the alert is still valid, it will be shown on the device immediately.

## Will ES-Alert replace the sirens and public address systems used in some areas to warn of specific risks (chemical plants or nuclear power stations, for example)?

No. ES-Alert is a system that complements, but does not replace, the current warning systems using sirens or public address. In the areas where these systems are deployed, ES-Alert messages will be received in addition to the sound signals from the traditional systems.
