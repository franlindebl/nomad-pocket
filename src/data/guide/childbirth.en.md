1. **Call the emergency number** and stay on the line if you can: they will guide you. Leave the door open.
2. **Prepare** a clean, soft place where the baby can't fall. Have dry towels, a blanket and a hat ready. Wash your hands; use gloves if you have them.
3. **Let it happen.** The mother picks a comfortable position, best not on her back, and pushes when she needs to.
4. **Gently support** the head and body as they come out. If the cord is around the neck, slip it over the head or loosen it.
5. **Dry the baby straight away** and remove the wet towel. Lay the baby naked on the mother's chest, skin to skin, cover both with dry towels and put a hat on. Keep the face clear.
6. **If the baby isn't crying or breathing**, rub the back or the soles of the feet. A bluish newborn is normal: watch the breathing. If still not breathing, lay the baby on its back, head straight, cover the mouth and nose with your mouth and give 5 gentle breaths, watching the chest rise. If no response, carry on with baby CPR.
7. **Don't pull the cord or rush to cut it.** If help is coming, leave it; if not, wait until it stops pulsing, tie it tightly in two places and cut between them with boiled scissors.
8. **The placenta** comes out by itself as the mother pushes. If it isn't out within an hour, she needs medical help.
9. **Put the baby to the breast** within the first hour.
10. **After the placenta**, firmly rub the belly below the navel until the womb feels hard and she bleeds less. If she can, have her pee: it helps the womb contract. She shouldn't get up alone: she may faint and fall.
11. **Call again** if she soaks one pad or more in an hour, passes clots bigger than an egg, or feels faint, faints or has a pounding heart. Meanwhile, lay her on her back with feet slightly raised, cover her and keep rubbing the womb.
