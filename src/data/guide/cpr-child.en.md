For babies, children and teenagers; not for a newborn at birth. If you can't tell whether it is a teenager or an adult, follow adult CPR.

1. **Safety.** Make sure the place is safe for you and the child.
2. **Do they respond?** Talk to them and touch them. Don't hurt them to see if they react.
3. **Call** the emergency number straight away, or have someone call. Put the speaker on, with video if you can, and follow the call handler's advice.
4. **Are they breathing normally?** Open the airway: one hand on the forehead and lift the chin with two fingers. For a baby, keep the head straight; for a child, tilt it a little further back; for a teenager, fully, as for an adult. Look for chest movement and listen and feel for air, for no more than 10 seconds. If in doubt, act as if they are not breathing.

## If they don't respond and aren't breathing normally

5. **5 rescue breaths.** Blow steadily for 1 second into their mouth (for a baby, cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth), just enough to make the chest rise. Let it fall and repeat. If the chest doesn't rise, remove anything you can see in the mouth if it is easy, without putting a finger in blindly, and reposition the head.
6. **Compressions**, on a firm surface if one is at hand, on the lower half of the breastbone:
   - **Baby (under 1 year):** wrap your hands around the chest and press with both thumbs.
   - **Child:** with the heel of one hand or with both hands, depending on their size.
   - Push down by at least one third of the depth of the chest, never more than 6 cm, 100 to 120 times a minute. Let the chest come all the way back up.
7. **Alternate 30 compressions and 2 breaths** without stopping. If you have done a paediatric life support course, use 15 compressions and 2 breaths.
8. If you can't or won't give breaths, give compressions only.
9. **Defibrillator (AED):** if someone else is there, have them call and bring an AED. If you are alone, calling and starting CPR come before fetching one. Switch it on and follow its instructions. If the child weighs less than 25 kg (about 8 years), switch on the child mode if it has one, and place one pad in the middle of the chest, just left of the breastbone, and the other on the back, between the shoulder blades. If it has no child mode, use it in normal mode. Don't touch the child while the AED is analysing, and go back to compressions right after the shock.
10. If you are alone and have no phone, give 1 minute of CPR before going for help.
11. **Don't stop** until help arrives or there are clear signs of life: they move, open their eyes or breathe normally. If they are breathing well but don't respond, put them on their side (unless they have had a hard knock) and check their breathing at least every minute.
