1. **Stop using it and stop charging it.** Unplug it. A swollen battery is damaged and can catch fire.
2. **If it smokes, hisses or is on fire, get away.** Warn everyone and leave the home, close the door and call 112 from outside. Don't try to put it out and don't move it while it is smoking: a lithium fire needs a lot of water and a small extinguisher is not enough.
3. **If it is only swollen and there is no immediate danger of fire:** don't puncture it, press it, hit it or bend it.
4. **Keep it somewhere safe** (if it is hot but not smoking, take it outdoors without touching it, for example with a shovel), away from anything that burns: in a bucket of sand or cat litter, or in another place away from flammable materials. Don't leave it on a bed, sofa or near paper or curtains.
5. **Don't put it in the household rubbish or the recycling bin.** Lithium batteries can cause fires in transport and at waste plants. Take it to a recycling point (punto limpio), or ask the manufacturer or the shop where you bought it.
6. If you take it to be recycled, cover the terminals with insulating tape or put it in a plastic bag on its own, so it doesn't touch other batteries or metals.

## So it doesn't happen again

- Charge with the original charger or a certified one.
- Charge on a hard, flat surface without blocking the ventilation. Don't charge on beds or sofas.
- Don't charge or leave the battery in direct sun or in a hot car.
