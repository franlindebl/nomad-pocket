## If you can't hear well or can't speak

1. **Sign up now**, before you need it. Each region has its own system (an app such as "112 Accesible", or SMS to 112) and they usually ask you to register first: an SMS to 112 when you are not registered may get no answer. Ask your local deaf association.
2. **SVIsual** (CNSE), in Spain: sign-language video interpreting and a text chat with channels for 112, 091, 062 and 016. Free, 24 hours, registration needed.
3. **AlertCops** (Ministry of the Interior): a free app to alert the National Police and the Guardia Civil by chat, with your location.
4. If someone is nearby, have them call for you.

## If you can't see and have no phone

1. Stop someone passing by, or go into a shop or a bar, and ask them to call 112.
2. Have them say where you are. Tell what you remember: the last street, a stop, a shop, what you can hear.
3. Stay in that place until help arrives.
4. Always carry your ID and a card with your medical details and a contact phone number.

## If you are bedridden and your carer collapses

1. Call 112. If you can't reach the phone but it's near, ask the voice assistant to call ("Hey Siri, call 112" / "Hey Google, call 112") and use the speaker.
2. Tell them your carer is on the floor, whether they respond and breathe, that you can't get up, and **how to get into the home**: who has a key, the door code.
3. No phone: press your personal alarm button if you have one; if not, shout and bang on the wall or the floor so the neighbours hear you.
4. Talk to your carer. Don't try to get up if you might fall.

## Plan ahead

- Leave a key with a neighbour you trust.
- Keep your phone charged and within reach of the bed.
- Ask your town council's social services for teleasistencia (a button that alerts a 24-hour call centre).
