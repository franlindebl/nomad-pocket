1. **Don't touch the person** while they are still in contact with the current or there may still be current: you would be electrocuted too.
2. **Cut off the power** if you can do it safely: unplug the appliance or switch off the mains or the breakers in the fuse box. Switching off the appliance alone may not stop the current.
3. **If you can't cut it off**, stand on something dry that doesn't conduct (a rubber mat, a wooden box, folded newspapers) and push the person away from the current with something dry made of wood or plastic, such as a broom handle. Never use anything wet or metal.
4. **High voltage** (overhead cables, power lines): don't try to rescue them and stay well away, and do not step towards downed cables, until the power has been cut off. Call the emergency number.
5. If a cable has fallen on your vehicle, stay inside until help arrives, unless there is a fire. If you have to get out, jump so that you never touch the vehicle and the ground at the same time.
6. **Call the emergency number** whenever someone has had an electric shock, even if it seems minor: they may have serious internal injuries, especially to the heart.

## Once there is no current

7. Check whether they respond and are breathing normally. **If they aren't breathing normally, start CPR** and use a defibrillator (AED) as soon as you have one.
8. Don't move them from where they are unless there is danger (fire, explosion). If they may have fallen, don't move their head or neck, except as needed for CPR.
9. **Burns:** remove any clothing that comes off easily and cool the area with cool running water. Don't put on ice, butter or ointments, cotton wool or plasters, and don't break blisters.
10. If they are pale or faint, lay them down and keep them warm with a blanket or a coat.
11. Stay with them until help arrives.
