**112 is free** all over Spain and the whole European Union, from landlines and mobiles. It works even if the phone is locked, has no credit, no active SIM card or no coverage from your own provider, as long as another one has coverage.

## When to call

- When there is real danger to people, homes or the surroundings: someone injured or very ill, a fire, someone trapped, a crime.
- **Don't call to ask what's happening**, about the roads or the weather: you hold up people who need it. Get information from the radio, ES-Alert warnings and your region's official 112 accounts.

## What to say

1. **Where.** Town, street, number, floor and door. If you don't know it, something nearby: a school, a stop. On a road, which road, the kilometre point or the last village, and which way you are going. In the countryside, read your coordinates on a map on your phone (GPS works without internet). Many phones send their position by themselves, but say it anyway.
2. **What has happened**: a fire, an accident, an assault… and how many people are affected.
3. **Your phone number.**
4. Answer their questions, do what they say and **don't hang up until they tell you to**. Then keep your phone free.

If you don't speak Spanish well, say in English or in your language which language you speak: they can help in English or through an interpreter.

## If nobody answers

- If it rings and nobody answers, **don't hang up**: wait. If the call drops, they will call you back.
- If it won't get through, try a direct number: 091 National Police, 062 Guardia Civil, 061 health emergencies (in some regions), your area's fire brigade (080 in many cities). Violence against women: 016 (in immediate danger, 112).

## No coverage

- No ringing tone means no network where you are: move and try again. Say where you are first, in case the call drops.
- If the network is down completely, ask for help in person: firefighters, police, Guardia Civil, a health centre or a patrol.
