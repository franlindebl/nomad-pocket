## If it happens while the children are at school

1. **If the authorities order people to shelter in place, don't go to fetch them.** They are better protected inside the building, and the teachers will get specific instructions. Going there puts you and them in more danger.
2. **If the school is evacuated**, the children will be taken to a safer place. Don't go to them during the emergency either.
3. **Don't phone** the school or the children: the lines are needed free for the emergency services. Follow official updates on the radio, the television or the internet.
4. **Wait** until the emergency is over or the school or the authorities say it is safe to pick them up. Then go to the place they tell you.
5. **To collect them, walk or cycle if you can:** the streets around schools may be jammed and the phones overloaded.

## If you are apart and have no way to talk

6. The school has an emergency plan to get the children back to their families. When it is safe, go to the school or to the pick-up place they have given.
7. If your plan has a meeting point, go there.

## Beforehand

8. Ask the school or nursery how they will tell families, how you can reach them in an emergency and where you will have to go to pick up the children.
9. Give them up-to-date contact details and a list of three people who can collect the children if you can't get there.
10. Put a card in the school bag with the child's name, date of birth, allergies or medical conditions, and the phone numbers of the school, yours and another contact in case you can't be reached.
11. Agree on a meeting point. If they are old enough, have them learn your phone numbers and another trusted adult's by heart.
