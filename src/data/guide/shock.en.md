## Position the casualty for shock

Move the casualty under a permanent or improvised shelter to shade them from direct sunlight. If the casualty is conscious, place the casualty in the shock recovery position (see figure 7-1), which is on their back with their feet elevated slightly above the level of their heart. This will help their blood circulation.

[…]

If a sitting position makes it easier for the casualty to breathe, that is fine.

The following steps will also significantly help in the prevention of shock:

- Loosen clothing at the neck, waist, or anywhere is binding.

**WARNING**

Do not loosen clothing if in a chemically contaminated area.

- Prevent the casualty from getting chilled or overheated. Using a blanket or clothing, cover the casualty to avoid loss of body heat by wrapping the blanket completely around the casualty.
- Note. Ensure no part of the casualty is touching the ground, as this increases loss of body heat.
- Calm and reassure the casualty by—
- Taking charge and showing self-confidence.
- Assuring the casualty that they are being taken care of.
- Watch the casualty closely for life-threatening conditions and check for other injuries, if necessary.
- Seek medical aid.

**WARNING**

If the casualty has a fractured lower extremity, do not place the casualty in the shock position until the fracture has been splinted.

## Food or drink

When providing first aid for shock, DO NOT give the casualty any food or drink. If you must leave the casualty or if they are unconscious, turn their head to the side (see figure 7-2) to prevent them from choking, if they vomit.
