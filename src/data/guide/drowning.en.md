## Rescue without putting yourself in danger

1. **Don't go into the water** unless you are trained in water rescue: you could drown too.
2. **Call the emergency number**, or have someone call, before you try anything.
3. **Help from land:** throw them something that floats, such as a lifebuoy, or reach out with a pole or a rope.
4. **Get them out of the water** as soon as you can without putting yourself at risk. If they need resuscitation, don't waste time keeping the neck still.

## If they don't respond and aren't breathing normally

5. **5 rescue breaths:** lay them face up, tilt the head back, lift the chin and blow into the mouth just enough to make the chest start to rise. For a baby, keep the head straight and cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth.
6. **CPR:** carry on with 30 compressions in the centre of the chest, 100 to 120 a minute, and 2 breaths. Push the chest down 5–6 cm in an adult and by one third of the chest in a child or a baby. Repeat without stopping until help arrives or they start breathing.
7. If you can't or won't give breaths, give compressions only, without stopping.
8. If there is a defibrillator (AED), dry the chest, attach it and follow its instructions. Don't delay CPR for the AED.
9. If there are several of you, have someone who didn't do the rescue give CPR: tiredness makes it worse.

## If they are breathing

10. If they don't respond but are breathing normally, put them in the recovery position, especially if they may vomit, and keep watching their breathing.
11. **Keep them from getting cold:** gently take off wet clothes, dry them, cover them with blankets or dry clothes, shield them from the wind and insulate them from the ground. Move them gently.
