## The mother

Up to a year after the birth, these signs can be serious. Emergency department now; 112 if she bleeds heavily, passes out, is confused, has fits, has chest pain or is short of breath:

- **Fever** of 38 °C or more, belly or stitch pain, bad-smelling discharge, or a red, swollen, painful breast that is getting worse.
- **Bleeding** that is increasing: soaking a pad in an hour, or clots bigger than an egg.
- **Chest pain**, shortness of breath or a racing heart.
- **One leg or arm** swollen and painful, red or warm.
- **A headache** that is severe or won't go away, blurred vision or flashing lights, or a very swollen face or hands.
- **Thoughts of harming herself or the baby**, or seeing or hearing things that aren't there, being very confused or elated, or not sleeping at all.


## The baby: yellow skin (jaundice)

Look in daylight at the whites of the eyes and the gums; it is harder to see on dark skin.

- **Have the baby seen today** by the paediatrician or the emergency department if you see yellow or if the pee is dark or the poo pale.
- **Emergency department or 112 now** if the yellow appeared in the first 24 hours of life, if the yellow reaches the palms of the hands or the soles of the feet, or if the baby is also very sleepy, not feeding, floppy or stiff, or making odd movements.
- If the baby is still yellow after 2 weeks (3 if born early), see the paediatrician.

## The baby: other danger signs (emergency department or 112)

A seriously ill baby may have no fever.

- Not feeding or no wet nappies.
- Breathing very fast (more than 60 breaths a minute), grunting, or the skin sucking in between the ribs.
- A temperature of 38 °C or more, or below 36 °C.
- Pale, grey, bluish or mottled, very floppy or not responding.
- Fits (seizures), or green or forceful, repeated vomiting.
- Spots on the skin that don't fade when pressed, a bulging soft spot (fontanelle) or a stiff neck.

Belly button red or with pus: to the paediatrician today; to the emergency department if the redness spreads.
