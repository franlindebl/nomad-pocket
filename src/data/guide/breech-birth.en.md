If the first thing you see is the bottom or the feet, not the head, the baby is breech.

1. **Call the emergency number**, or call again, and say the baby is breech. Follow their instructions.
2. **Move the mother** so her bottom is right at the edge of the bed, knees drawn up towards her chest. Put pillows or blankets on the floor below in case the baby drops.
3. **Don't touch or pull the baby.** Let the bottom, legs, body and arms come out by themselves with the pushes. The baby's back will face you.
4. **Let the baby's body hang down** freely towards the floor; don't hold it up, even if it looks like it might fall. If it's hard to keep your hands off, add another pillow underneath.
5. **Once the head is out**, lift the baby, holding it under the arms, onto the mother's chest.
6. **If the arms are born and the head hasn't followed** by the next contraction, help the mother off the bed into a squat and have her push.
7. **Once born**, dry the baby straight away, lay it naked on the mother's chest, skin to skin, cover both with dry towels and put a hat on. Keep the face clear.
8. **If the baby isn't crying or breathing**, rub the back or the soles of the feet. If still not breathing, lay the baby on its back, head straight, cover the mouth and nose with your mouth and give 5 gentle breaths, watching the chest rise. If no response, carry on with baby CPR.
9. **Don't pull the cord.** The placenta comes out by itself, as in any birth.
