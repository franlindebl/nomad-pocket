A nearby explosion can damage the ears, lungs and belly even when no wound can be seen. Some signs take time to appear: in the lungs, up to 48 hours.

## Call 112 even if they seem fine if

- they are short of breath, coughing, coughing up blood or have chest pain;
- they have belly pain, feel sick or are vomiting, above all if they vomit blood;
- they are confused, very drowsy, have a bad headache or vomit after a blow: head injuries are easily missed;
- they are bleeding heavily, or are pale, cold and sweaty;
- something has got into an eye or they can't see well;
- they are more than about 3 months pregnant (second or third trimester): she should be watched in a hospital.

## Look for small wounds

Check the whole body, under the clothes too. Fragments and debris thrown by the blast can make small wounds that bleed little but go deep. Don't pull out anything stuck in.

## The ears

- Ringing, muffled hearing, earache or dizziness are common after an explosion.
- Most people hear well again within a few hours or 1 or 2 days; sometimes it takes days or weeks.
- Don't put anything in the ear (cotton buds, drops) or let water in: keep it clean and dry.
- **See a doctor** if blood or fluid comes from the ear, if it hurts, if there is dizziness or vertigo, or if hearing isn't better after 1 or 2 days. Hearing loss isn't always noticed: everyone who was nearby should have their ears checked.
- Speak to someone who can't hear well face to face and slowly, or write the instructions down.
