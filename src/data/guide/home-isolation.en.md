## Isolating a sick person in a small home

1. They stay in one room with the door shut and air it often. Keep there their mobile, tissues, hand gel or soap and a lidded bin with a bag.
2. One person only looks after them, if possible someone not at risk of complications.
3. They eat in their room. Towels, glasses, plates, cutlery and toothbrush for them only; wash them in hot water or in the dishwasher.
4. If there is only one bathroom, disinfect the toilet and whatever they touched before anyone else goes in, with diluted bleach (1 part bleach to 50 parts water), and air it.
5. The sick person wears a mask if they leave their room or someone comes in; the carer, whenever they share a space. Keep more than 2 metres apart.
6. Their clothes: don't shake them, put them in a closed bag and wash them at 60–90 °C. Wash your hands afterwards, even if you wore gloves.
7. Their rubbish: their bin bag, tied, inside another bag, into the general waste container.

## Taking food to someone who lives alone

- If you have symptoms, don't go: send someone else.
- Leave the shopping at the door, let them know by phone or the doorbell and step more than 2 metres back before they open.
- If you meet, better outdoors and with masks; if indoors, open the windows.

## If you are sick and the only one looking after your children

- Ask now for help from a relative, friend or neighbour who can look after them or leave food at your door. Better to agree it in advance.
- Near them, wear a mask, wash your hands often and air the room.
- If they are old enough, teach them to call 112 and the number of someone you trust. Explain that if you don't wake up or can't speak, they call 112 and tell a neighbour.
- Call your health centre if you get worse, and 112 straight away if you are short of breath, have pain or pressure in your chest or are very confused or dizzy.
