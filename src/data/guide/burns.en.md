## What to do with a burn

Immediate care of a burned person is very similar to that of any injured person, with the particularity that the first thing to do, without forgetting our own safety, is to stop the burning process; that is, ELIMINATE THE CAUSE.

1. Get the person away from the source of heat, put out the flames, remove the chemical from contact with the skin, … all of this to reduce the thermal injury.
2. Carry out an initial assessment and maintain the vital signs. The presence of inhalation burns or of poisoning by inhaling gases such as carbon monoxide or breakdown products during a fire must be detected as soon as possible.
3. Look for other possible injuries such as bleeding, shock, fractures. The most serious injury will always be treated first.
4. Cool the burned area: Apply plenty of WATER (20-30 minutes) to the burned surface, avoiding chilling the patient (risk of hypothermia). Remove clothing, jewellery and anything that keeps in the heat.
5. Wrap the injury in clean gauze or cloths, moistened with water. The bandage must be loose.
6. Take the person to a hospital with a Burns Unit, lying on their side, to avoid the consequences of vomiting (choking).

## What NOT to do with a burn

1. Apply ointments, creams, toothpaste, … to the burn. Only water.
2. Chill the patient too much, ONLY the burned area. If shivering appears or the burned area is more than 20 %, we must cover them with a thermal blanket.
3. Give water, alcohol, painkillers... by mouth.
4. Break the blisters, because the fluid they contain protects against possible infection. By breaking them we would open a door for germs to get in.
5. Pull off clothing or anything else that is stuck to the skin.
6. Leave the victim alone. If we have to go for help, we will take them with us, as long as their injuries allow it.
7. Delay transport. At the scene of the accident we cannot stabilise the victim clinically, and the chance of them going into shock increases the larger the burned area is.
