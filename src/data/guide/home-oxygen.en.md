## If the power goes out

1. **Switch to the backup cylinder at once**, at the same flow you were prescribed. Don't turn the flow up on your own.
2. **Call the company that supplies your oxygen** and tell them how much you have left. Keep their number written on paper.
3. **Portable concentrator:** it runs on its battery while the battery lasts. Check the user manual or ask the supplier whether it can run on spare batteries or a generator, ideally before you need to.
4. **If the backup will run out before the power comes back**, don't wait until you have no oxygen: go in good time to a hospital or another place with electricity, or call 112.
5. **Call 112** if breathing gets harder, the lips turn bluish or grey, or the person is confused.

## Oxygen feeds fire

Oxygen doesn't burn, but it makes whatever is near it burn much faster.

- If there is a fire, close the cylinder valve or switch off the concentrator straight away, and keep the oxygen source and the tubing away from the flames.
- Nobody smokes near the oxygen.
- No candles, matches, lighters or gas lamps: use battery torches.
- If a room has a heater on (electric, gas or butane) or anything that gives off sparks, the oxygen has to be in another room.
- While wearing the oxygen, don't go near a fire or a gas hob to cook.
- Keep cylinders away from the sun and from any source of heat.
- The concentrator: in a well-ventilated room, at least 15 cm from the wall or furniture, never covered, always upright when moved, and plugged straight into the wall, with no adapters or extension strips.
- Never grease the equipment or the cylinder valve, or clean them with oil.
- Don't put petroleum jelly, hairspray or greasy creams on your face or nose while wearing nasal prongs; if your nose gets dry, use a water-based moisturiser.
