1. **Sit them upright.** Don't let them lie down. Stay calm and help them calm down.
2. **Reliever inhaler** (the blue one, salbutamol, such as Ventolin). If they have an asthma action plan, follow the dose in their plan. If not: **1 puff every 30 to 60 seconds, up to 10 puffs at most.** Shake the inhaler between puffs. The same for children.
3. **With a spacer** if they have one (with a mask for young children). Without a spacer, a 500 ml plastic bottle with a hole in the bottom that fits tightly around the inhaler's mouthpiece can work: breathe through the bottle's neck.
4. **If they don't have their inhaler**, in an emergency they can use someone else's blue salbutamol inhaler: it is safe. Call 112.

## Call 112 now if

- They have no inhaler, they're no better after 10 puffs, or they get worse at any time.
- They can't talk, walk or eat because they're short of breath.
- Their lips are turning blue or they are very pale.
- Their chest sinks in or their ribs show with each breath, and they breathe faster and faster.
- They are drowsy, confused or very agitated.

While help is on its way: if they're no better after 10 minutes, repeat up to 10 more puffs, the same way. If they still don't improve and nobody has arrived, call 112 again.

Even if they recover, they should see a doctor within the next 2 days.

## If there is smoke from a fire

- Get them away from the smoke: indoors with doors and windows shut, or out of the area if you can leave safely.
- Keep the inhaler at hand and follow their action plan.
- An FFP2 mask only helps if it fits the face well, and from age 2. Surgical masks, scarves or a wet cloth don't protect against smoke.

## Have ready

- The reliever inhaler and spacer, always on them and in date.
- A spare one in the emergency bag, with the rest of their medicines.
