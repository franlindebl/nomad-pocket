During a radiation emergency, get inside as soon as possible.

The walls of your home can block much of the harmful radiation, and radioactive materials become weaker over time. Staying inside for at least 24 hours can protect you and your family until it is safe to leave the area.

## If you are indoors during a radiation emergency

- Stay inside.
- Close and lock all windows and doors.
- Go to the basement or the middle of the building.
- Stay as far away from the walls and roof of the building as you can. Radiation settles on the outside of buildings.
- If possible, turn off fans, air conditioners, and forced-air heating units that bring air in from the outside.
- Close fireplace dampers.

## If you are outside or in a vehicle

- Get inside a building right away. Brick or concrete multi-story building or basement are best, but being inside any building is safer than being outside.
- Carefully remove your outer layer of clothing before entering the building, if you can. Radioactive material can settle on your clothing and your body, like dust or mud.
- Once inside, go to the basement or the middle of the building.
- Wash the parts of your body that were uncovered when you were outside. Then put on clean clothing, if you can.
- If you must be outside and cannot get inside immediately, cover your mouth and nose with a mask, cloth, or towel.

## If you have loved ones in other places

Stay where you are! Going outside to get loved ones could expose you and them to dangerous levels of radiation.

## Stay inside

Always listen for additional instructions from emergency officials and radiation experts.

### Water, food, and medicine safety

- Foods and drinks that are sealed or that were already inside are safe.
- Boiling tap water will not remove radioactive materials.
- If your medication was inside, continue taking it. If the container was outside, wipe off the container and continue taking it.
