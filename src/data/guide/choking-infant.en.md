Suspect choking if the baby suddenly can't cry or cough, especially while feeding or playing.

1. **Get help.** Have someone call the emergency number, with the speaker on. If you are alone, call as soon as you can and, at the latest, when the baby stops responding.
2. **If the baby is coughing hard**, let them cough and watch them.

## If conscious but can't cough or cry

3. **5 back blows.** Lay the baby face down along your forearm, your arm resting on your leg and the baby's head lower than the chest. Support the head with your hand. Give up to 5 sharp blows between the shoulder blades.
4. **5 chest thrusts.** If the object hasn't come out, turn the baby face up on your knees. Wrap your hands around the chest and press with both thumbs on the lower half of the breastbone, more sharply than in CPR. Up to 5 times.
5. **Repeat** 5 blows and 5 thrusts while the baby is conscious. **Stop** as soon as they cough, cry or breathe noisily.
6. Don't put a finger blindly into the mouth. Remove the object only if you can see it clearly, in a single sweep.

## If the baby stops responding

7. Call the emergency number if nobody has.
8. Lay the baby face up on a firm surface. Keep the head straight, not tilted back, and lift the chin with two fingers.
9. **5 rescue breaths:** cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth and blow steadily for 1 second, just enough to make the chest rise. If it doesn't rise, remove anything you can see in the mouth and reposition the head.
10. **Compressions:** with both thumbs, hands around the chest, push the lower half of the breastbone down by one third of the chest, 100 to 120 times a minute.
11. Alternate 30 compressions and 2 breaths without stopping until help arrives or the baby responds.
