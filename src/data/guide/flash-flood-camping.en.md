**If it is raining hard and you are camping by a watercourse, don't wait to see the water rise: leave now for high ground.** A flash flood can arrive within minutes, even under sunny skies, from a storm falling far away upstream that you can't see.

## Signs that a flash flood is coming

- The water turns from clear to muddy or brown.
- Branches, logs or other debris float down.
- The water level rises or the current gets stronger.
- A growing roar can be heard upstream.

## What to do

1. **Get out of the riverbed now.** Climb the slope to one side, to the highest point you can; don't keep going along the bottom of the ravine or dry riverbed.
2. **Leave the tent and the gear.** Don't waste time packing: the water can rise in minutes or seconds.
3. **Don't cross the water**, on foot or by car: about 15 cm of moving water can knock you down and 30–60 cm can carry a car away.
4. **Keep away from the low parts of slopes too.**
5. **Stay up high and be patient:** the water can take hours to fall to a safe level.
6. If someone is trapped by the water, they should climb to the highest point they can. Call 112.

## Before you go

- **Never camp in a ravine, a dry riverbed or next to a river channel**, even if it is dry. Pick a high spot, away from rivers, streams and low ground.
- Check the forecast and AEMET's warnings. If there is a rain or storm warning, change your plans.
- Carry your phone with alerts on and a radio: Protección Civil sends warnings through them.
- Tell someone where you are going and when you will be back.
