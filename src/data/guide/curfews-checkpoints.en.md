1. **If it's an emergency, call 112 first.** They can send help to where you are.
2. **Go out only if you need to** and go back home as soon as you are done.
3. **Carry your DNI, NIE or passport.** The police can ask you to identify yourself, and refusing is an offence. Also carry whatever justifies the trip, if you have it: a medical report, a prescription, a certificate from work.

## What is allowed

4. **Each declaration says.** In a state of alarm, the Government can limit the movement of people and vehicles at certain hours and in certain places, or allow it only if some requirements are met. In a state of exception it can ban it and require anyone travelling to prove their identity and follow the route they are given.
5. **Emergencies have been allowed.** During the 2020 curfew people could go out, among other reasons, to go to a health centre, buy medicines, care for older people, children or dependent people, take an animal to the vet in an emergency, and for force majeure or a situation of need. Check the exceptions in the rules in force.
6. **Get your information from official channels:** radio, television, ES-Alert and the Government's and the BOE's websites.

## At a checkpoint

7. **Stop** when told to and obey the officers' orders. Disobeying or resisting is punished, and can be a crime.
8. **Keep your hands visible** and empty.
9. **Explain calmly** where you are going and why, and show your ID and your proof. If the emergency is serious, say so as soon as you get there.
