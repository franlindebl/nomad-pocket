In Spain there are currently seven nuclear reactors in operation, located at five sites:
Almaraz I and II, in the province of Cáceres, Trillo, in the province of Guadalajara, Vandellós II, Ascó I and Ascó II, in the province of Tarragona and Cofrentes, in the province of Valencia.

## Planning zones

Zone I, or urgent protection measures zone, is the circle with a radius of 10 km, concentric with the nuclear power plant, which includes zone 0.

Zone II, or long-term protection measures zone, is the circular ring between the circles with radii of 10 and 30 km, concentric with the nuclear power plant, in which the routes of exposure to radiation are mainly associated with the radioactive material deposited on the ground after the accident.

In the event of a real accident, depending on its severity and on the weather conditions, the protection measures may be limited to part of the planning zones or extended beyond them.

The municipalities of zone I will be those with all or part of their inhabited municipal area in zone I.

Their municipal action plan in a nuclear emergency will be suited to carrying out the following functions:

- Facilitate the distribution of the substances for radiological prophylaxis.
- Facilitate sheltering and supplying the sheltered population.
- Facilitate the evacuation of the population, if necessary.

The municipalities of zone II will be those with all or part of their inhabited municipal area in zone II and that do not belong to zone I.

The directors of the PAMEN will promote and facilitate citizen participation in the drills.

## In an emergency

Within a radius of 10 km of the nuclear power plant, there are also loudspeakers to give warnings of the measures to be taken to the resident population.

Staying sheltered makes it easier to take potassium iodide when the health authorities say so.

If the authorities order an evacuation, it is essential to take documents, medicines and other indispensable belongings, making sure to turn off the water, gas and other supplies.
