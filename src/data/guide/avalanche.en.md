Every minute counts: **the first 15 minutes are the best chance** of saving them. Do not wait for rescue teams.

1. **Check for another slide.** If the slope above you is not stable, move the group out of the danger zone and do not send anyone alone onto an unstable slope: your own safety comes first.
2. **Watch and remember.** Note the point where you last saw them and the direction the snow carried them. Mark both (a ski, a pole, a piece of clothing).
3. **Call 112** as soon as you can. Ask for mountain rescue. If more than one person is unhurt, one calls while the others search. If you are alone, search first, but call as soon as you locate the person or get signal, without waiting longer.
4. **Search.** With an avalanche transceiver: everyone unhurt switches to search mode and follows the signal; when close, pinpoint with the probe and leave it in place. Without a transceiver: search the snow, below the point where you last saw them, and look for a hand, clothing or gear showing.
5. **Dig fast.** With a shovel, start digging to one side of the probe, from below. Throw the snow out and take turns when you tire. **Free the head and airway first.**
6. **When you see the face,** clear the mouth and nose and check whether they are breathing.
7. **If they are not breathing,** start CPR with 5 rescue breaths and keep going until help arrives (see adult CPR), even if they feel cold; use a defibrillator if there is one.
8. **If they are breathing,** keep them warm and handle them gently. If they are unconscious but breathing, place them in the recovery position. Even if they seem fine, a medical team must check them: they may be chilled or have injuries you cannot see. Do not leave them alone.

**For next time:** read the AEMET avalanche danger bulletin and stay home if the danger is high. Always carry a transceiver, probe and shovel, and know how to use them.
