After a disaster, people appear who pretend to be technicians, officials or volunteers to get into homes or collect money.

## If someone knocks at your door

1. **Do not open.** Talk through the door.
2. Ask them to show identification and say why they came. A genuine company warns you before a visit.
3. **Call the company or the council yourself** on its official number (the one on your bill or its website). Do not use a phone number given by the person at the door.
4. If you cannot confirm it, do not let them in. Missing a visit is better than letting a stranger in.
5. **Never** let anyone in, pay cash or sign anything on the spot, however much they rush you.
6. If they insist, turn aggressive or do not leave, call **112** or 091.

## Other common scams

- Aid, donations or repairs offered in exchange for payment in advance.
- Text messages, calls or emails asking for your details, your account or money "to receive the aid". Official aid is not requested this way: do not tap links or give passwords.
- Offers to repair the house or clean up at a very low price, paid in advance.

## Warn others

Tell your neighbours, especially older people living alone, and ask them not to open the door to anyone without checking.
