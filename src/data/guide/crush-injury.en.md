1. **Call 112** (or have someone call): give the exact place, say a person is trapped and **since when**. Follow their instructions.
2. **Make the scene safe.** With a tractor, car or machine, if you can do it without danger, switch the engine off, take the key out and put the handbrake on. No one should smoke nearby. Check that nothing can move, tip over or fall further.
3. **Don't lift or lever heavy weights** (a tractor, beams, a wall): if they shift, they can crush the person more or trap you.
4. **Taking the weight off early is best**, if it's safe: if it's something light you can move without anything else shifting, move it now.

## If they have been trapped a long time

5. Tell 112. When the weight comes off they can suddenly get worse (dizziness, paleness, fainting, even the heart stopping). That is why medics give them fluids before freeing them. It can happen after hours trapped, and sometimes after less than an hour.
6. **If rescue teams are going to arrive, let them free the person.**
7. **If no one can come**, free them carefully as soon as it's safe: the sooner, the better. Afterwards keep them lying down and warm, watch them without leaving them, and get them to hospital as soon as possible **even if they seem fine**: kidney damage can appear hours later.

## Meanwhile

8. Talk to them, reassure them and check they are breathing.
9. If they are bleeding heavily, press on the wound. Don't put on a tourniquet to "hold back" the crush: only if severe bleeding won't stop.
10. Keep them warm, against cold and shock.
11. If they could have a back or neck injury (rollover, fall, hard blow), don't move them unless there is danger such as fire; if you must, move head, neck and body together, as one block.
12. Give them nothing to eat or drink, unless 112 tells you to.
13. If they don't respond and aren't breathing normally, tell 112 and start CPR if you can reach them.
