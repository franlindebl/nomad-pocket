1. **Call 112** if you see a cloud, notice a strange smell, or people have stinging eyes, a cough or trouble breathing. Follow what the authorities say on the radio, TV or loudspeakers.
2. **Don't wait to smell something.** Some toxic chemicals have no smell. A smell of garlic, onion or mustard can be mustard gas, whose symptoms can take up to 24 hours to appear.

## If you are outside

3. **If there is a building nearby, go in** and shelter with doors and windows closed. On the way, breathe through a damp cloth.
4. **If not, get away at once to clean air**, to a place where the wind blows from you towards the leak, not from the leak towards you. Never go where the wind is carrying the cloud, through the cloud, or past the leak. To tell which way the wind blows, look where smoke drifts or flags fly.
5. **If you can't get away, go to the highest ground you can.** Chlorine, sarin and mustard gas are heavier than air and collect low down.

## Inside a building

6. **If the leak is inside the building, get out** without passing through the contaminated area. If you can't, move as far away from it as you can and shelter there.
7. **If the cloud comes from outside, don't go down to the basement.** Choose an interior room on an upper floor, away from windows.
8. **If the windows are broken** (by an explosion, for example), go to an interior room on the side away from the broken windows. Close the door and block the gaps with wet towels, plastic or tape.
9. **Don't go out** until the authorities say it is safe. If they order an evacuation, leave at once and follow the route and meeting point they give.

## If the substance reached you

10. Take off your clothes without pulling them over your head (cut them off if needed) and put them in a sealed bag. Take out contact lenses, rinse your eyes with water and wash with soap and water, without scrubbing. As soon as it is safe, go to a medical centre to be checked.
