## If the car stops

1. If it can still move, get it out of the lanes: to the first exit or onto the right-hand hard shoulder.
2. Turn on the hazard lights.
3. Switch on the V-16 beacon and place it from inside, on the roof. Since 1 January 2026 it is the only valid warning device; triangles no longer are.
4. Put on the hi-vis vest before you get out.
5. Get out on the side away from the traffic only if there is a safe place off the road (for example, behind the crash barrier) and wait there, never on the hard shoulder or the carriageway. If it isn't safe to get out, stay inside with your seatbelt on.
6. In a tunnel: switch off the engine, call for help at the SOS post and walk to the nearest refuge or exit, keeping off the carriageway.

## Getting help

- The V-16 doesn't call anyone. Call your insurer's roadside assistance yourself.
- If someone is hurt or in danger, call 112.
- With no signal from your own network, 112 can still get through if another network covers the area. If there is no network at all, the call won't go out.

## If nobody comes

- Stay with the car: rescuers spot a car sooner than a person walking.
- If you decide to go on foot, leave a note in the car saying where you are going and when you left, keep off the carriageway and wear the vest.

## In snow

- Stay inside the car.
- Clear snow from the exhaust pipe to avoid carbon monoxide.
- Run the engine about 10 minutes each hour for heat, with a window open a little.
- At night, while the engine runs, turn on the inside light. Tie a brightly coloured cloth to the aerial or the door.
- When the snow stops, raise the bonnet to show you need help.

## For next time

Keep water, something to eat, a blanket, warm clothes, a torch and a car phone charger in the car.
