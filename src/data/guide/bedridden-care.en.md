1. **Change their position every 1 to 2 hours**: on their back, on one side, on the other side.
2. **Don't drag them** across the sheet. To move them or shift them up the bed, lift them with two people or slide them on a folded sheet placed under them (draw sheet).
3. **Take the pressure off with pillows**: on their side, one between the knees and ankles. On their back, one under the calves so the heels are off the bed, and others under the lower back, shoulders and elbows. Don't put pillows under the knees: it loads the heels.
4. **Don't raise the head of the bed more than 30 degrees**: if it is steeper, they slide down and the skin gets damaged.
5. **Check their skin every day**, especially heels, ankles, knees, hips, tailbone, elbows, shoulders, shoulder blades, back of the head and ears. The first warning is a red or purple patch that doesn't change colour when pressed, or skin that is warm, spongy or hard. Don't let them lie on that spot.
6. **Clean, dry skin**: if they wet or soil themselves, clean them straight away and dry them well. Wash gently, without scrubbing, and with no talc or strong soaps. Sheets dry and smooth, with no wrinkles or objects in the bed. Clothes without thick seams, buttons or zips that press.
7. **Make sure they drink water and eat enough**, with protein.
8. **If they can, they should move their feet and legs often.** A swollen, painful leg, in the calf or thigh, may be a blood clot: get urgent medical help. If they are also short of breath or have chest pain, call 112.

## When to get medical help

Call their health centre or ask for a nurse to come if you see:

- a blister, an open sore or black skin;
- hot, swollen or red skin around the sore, or pus;
- a fever, or severe or worsening pain;
- redness that doesn't go away in a few days.
