If you smell gas (natural gas or a butane or propane bottle), act now. A spark can cause an explosion.

1. **Make no sparks or flames.** Don't switch lights on or off, don't touch switches, doorbells or plugs, don't use the lift, and don't light matches, lighters or burners. Don't use your phone inside the building.
2. **Open doors and windows** if you can do it quickly and safely, so the gas can get out.
3. **Close the gas valve** (the one for the home or for the bottle) if you can reach it safely. For the home valve, turn a quarter turn until it is crosswise to the pipe. For the bottle, turn the regulator clockwise until it stops.
4. **Leave the building** with everyone, and with pets if you can. Don't waste time collecting things.
5. **Warn neighbours by knocking on their doors**, not by ringing the bell.
6. **Call 112 from outside**, away from the building, and call the gas company too. Give the exact address and say there is a smell of gas.
7. **Don't go back in** until the fire service or the gas technicians say it is safe.

## After a bombing or an earthquake

Do the same. Earthquakes and explosions can break gas pipes. If you smell gas in the stairwell, go out that way without touching switches and call from outside. If the building is damaged, close the gas only if you can do it safely.

## If you feel unwell

Headache, dizziness, nausea or drowsiness: get into fresh air now and call 112.

## Turning the gas back on

Only the gas company or an approved installer may check the installation and turn it back on. Don't do it yourself.
