## Call 112 if she

- has a seizure,
- faints and doesn't wake up within 1 minute,
- bleeds heavily, has severe pain in the tummy, struggles to breathe or has chest pain.

## If she has a seizure (eclampsia)

1. Call 112. Move away anything that could hurt her and put something soft under her head. Don't hold her down or put anything in her mouth.
2. Gently turn her on her side, preferably the left. If she isn't breathing normally when it ends, start CPR.

## If she faints

1. Lay her on her side, preferably the left, not on her back: on her back, the womb presses on a large blood vessel.
2. Check whether she is bleeding, has tummy pain or is having a seizure: if so, 112.
3. Even if she recovers, she should see a doctor today.

## Pre-eclampsia: go to hospital now

A severe headache that painkillers don't ease, blurred vision or flashing lights, pain below the ribs, sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet, feeling very unwell or vomiting. It can cause seizures or a stroke. If you can't get to the hospital, call 112.

## Contractions before 37 weeks

Regular contractions (every 10 minutes or more often), period-like pain, pressure in the pelvis, unusual backache or leaking fluid: go to the hospital now or call 112. They can't be stopped at home. In hospital they can try to slow them and give medicines that protect the baby's lungs and brain. If the baby is born, it gets cold very quickly: dry it and put it skin to skin on the mother, both covered with dry towels, with a hat on.

## Go to hospital straight away, even at night, if

- the baby is moving less than usual or you can't feel it (don't rely on a home doppler),
- you are leaking fluid or blood,
- you have a fever of 38 °C or more,
- your skin is very itchy, especially the palms and soles, or worse at night: it can be cholestasis, a liver problem that needs treatment.
