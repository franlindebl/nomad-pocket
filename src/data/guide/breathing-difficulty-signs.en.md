## Call 112 now if

- They are gasping, choking or can't get words out.
- Their lips, tongue or skin are turning blue, grey or very pale (on dark skin, look at the palms of the hands).
- They are suddenly confused, very drowsy or hard to wake. A child who is floppy, not responding as usual.
- Their chest feels tight, or pain spreads to the arms, back, neck or jaw.
- A child grunts when breathing, or their tummy sucks in under the ribs.
- A baby has pauses in their breathing.
- A child is drooling more than usual, can't swallow or only wants to sit up.

## How to tell without a pulse oximeter

1. **Can they talk?** If they can't get words out for lack of breath, it is serious.
2. **Look at the colour** of lips and tongue, and whether they are awake and alert as usual.
3. **Look at the chest:** in a child, whether it sinks in below or between the ribs when breathing in (chest indrawing).
4. **Listen:** a high-pitched sound when breathing in while the child is calm (stridor), wheezing or grunting.
5. **Count the breaths** for 1 full minute, at rest (with a child, calm). Each time the chest rises, one. Fast is:
   - under 2 months: 60 or more
   - 2 to 12 months: 50 or more
   - 1 to 5 years: 40 or more
   - adults: more than 20 (normal is 12 to 20); 25 or more is serious.

## While help is on its way

- If they are awake, keep them sitting up straight if that helps them breathe; don't lay them down.
- Child with a barking cough and a noise when breathing in (croup): sitting up straight; comfort them and keep them calm, because crying makes it worse. Don't use steam.
- Baby with a runny nose, cough and fast breathing (bronchiolitis): smaller feeds, more often. Get urgent medical help if they feed much less than normal or have had a dry nappy for 12 hours or more.
- Check on them often, at night too: if any of the signs above appears, call 112.
