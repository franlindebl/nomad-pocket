From 1 year, a child can gradually eat the same as the family. Prepare it soft, mashed with a fork or in small pieces, and with little salt.

## What to give from the pantry

- **Tinned pulses** (chickpeas, lentils, beans): rinsed and mashed.
- **Tinned fish** (sardines, mackerel, tuna): rinsed, flaked and boneless. No swordfish, bluefin tuna, shark (dogfish) or pike.
- **Tinned vegetables** (peas, carrots, green beans): rinsed, mashed or in small pieces.
- **Tinned fruit**, better in its own juice than in syrup, in small pieces.
- **Well-cooked rice or pasta, bread without seeds, oat flakes or cereal with no sugar**, soft or soaked in milk.
- **Pasteurised cheese** in small pieces, **cooked egg**, **baby food jars**.

Rinsing tinned food takes salt off; if you can, choose "no added salt". Avoid cured meats and sausages.

## How much

Their stomach is small: 3 meals and 2 or 3 snacks a day, in small portions. Let them decide how much to eat; it's normal for them to eat little on some days.

## Milk and water

- **From 1 year:** whole milk (UHT milk needs no fridge until opened). If they can't have it, a soy drink fortified with calcium and vitamin D, with no sugar; rice, almond or oat drinks don't replace milk. About 2 servings of dairy a day; too much milk takes their appetite away.
- **Before 1 year**, no cow's milk, no plant drinks and no honey.
- **Safe water** often: between drinks and food they need about 1.1–1.2 litres a day from 1 to 2 years and 1.3 litres from 2 to 3. No fizzy drinks, sugary juice drinks, sports drinks, tea or coffee; whole fruit is better than juice.

## So they don't choke

Always seated and watched. Don't give them whole or chopped nuts, sunflower and other seeds, popcorn, uncut grapes, cherries, berries or cherry tomatoes, sweetcorn kernels, raw carrot or apple, raisins, whole beans, large chunks of meat or cheese, sausages, biscuits, cereal bars, crisps, sweets or spoonfuls of peanut butter.

## Hygiene

- Wash your hands and theirs before eating.
- Serve on a plate, not from the tin or the jar, and throw away what they leave.
- With no fridge, what's left in an opened tin, milk carton or jar can't be kept more than 2 hours (1 hour above 32 °C): throw it away.
