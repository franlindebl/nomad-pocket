## Jellyfish

1. **Get out of the water** and tell the lifeguard or go to the first-aid post.
2. **Rinse the area without rubbing**, with sea water or saline solution. Never with fresh water: what is left of the jellyfish can release more venom.
3. **Don't rub or scratch**, not even with a towel or sand.
4. **Remove any bits of tentacle** with tweezers or with two cards used like tweezers. Never with your hand.
5. **Apply cold** afterwards, for up to 20 minutes: ice inside a plastic bag, never directly on the skin.
6. **Keep the area out of the sun.**
7. If the pain is very bad or you keep getting worse, tell the lifeguard again or go to a medical centre straight away.

The **Portuguese man o' war** (a transparent blue floating bag with a violet sail and tentacles 10 to 20 metres long) causes very strong pain and, rarely, breathing or heart problems: do the same and get medical care if the pain is strong or getting worse.

Watch children, people with allergies and anyone with heart problems more closely.

## Weever fish

It stings by driving its spines in.

1. **Rinse with plenty of water** to get the spines out.
2. **Put the area in hot water**, as hot as can be borne without scalding, for 30 to 60 minutes. Heat inactivates the venom. Careful: the pain stops you noticing if the water is too hot.
3. **Don't apply cold or ice.**
4. **Remove any spines left** with tweezers or gloves, without forcing them.
5. Keep the leg or arm **raised and at rest**.
6. Get medical care if it swells a lot, you have a fever or you feel unwell: nausea, vomiting, cramps, weakness or dizziness.

## Call 112

If there is difficulty breathing, swelling of the face, lips or tongue, hives all over the body, severe dizziness or loss of consciousness.
