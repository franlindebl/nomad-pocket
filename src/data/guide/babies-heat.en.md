## Get help now

- **Call 112** if they are floppy or limp, drowsy or confused, more irritable than usual, refuse the breast, the bottle or water, have sunken eyes or cry without tears, have pale or cold skin, or look unwell. Also if their skin is very hot and not sweating, they breathe very fast, have a seizure or lose consciousness. While help is on the way, cool them down.
- **Go to the emergency department or your health centre today** if they have fewer wet nappies or darker pee, or a sunken fontanelle (the soft spot on the head).

## Cool them down

1. Take them to the coolest room, in the shade. Close blinds and curtains during the day and open the windows at night, when it's cooler outside.
2. Take clothes off: light, loose, light-coloured clothing. In great heat, a short-sleeved vest or just a nappy is enough.
3. Wipe them with a damp cloth or a sponge with lukewarm water, or give them a lukewarm bath. Never cold water.
4. A fan helps move the air, but don't point it at the baby and keep it out of their reach.

## Give them fluids

5. If they only have breast milk, they don't need water: offer the breast more often. Whoever breastfeeds should drink plenty too.
6. If they are bottle-fed, they may need more feeds. You can also give them a little cooled boiled water.
7. If they already eat solid food, offer water often. Avoid very cold or very sugary drinks.

## Never

- Never leave them in a parked car, not even for a moment, even with the window open.
- Don't cover the pram with a blanket, muslin or cloth: it traps the heat and you can't see how they are. Use a clip-on sunshade. In hot weather, don't let them sleep in the pram.
- Under 6 months, no direct sun.
