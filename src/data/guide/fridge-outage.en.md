Each food should be assessed separately, following the recommendations below. Never taste a food to judge whether it is safe, and do not rely on how the food looks or smells.

What matters is the temperature each type of food has reached, depending on the preserving capacity of your fridge. The longer the time without electricity, the greater the risk of reaching an unsuitable temperature. The times given below assume that opening the fridge or freezer doors was avoided during the power cut.

## Recommendations for food in the fridge

- Power cuts of less than 4 hours: The fridge can keep food below 5 °C and, in principle, there would be no need to discard any food because of the power cut.
- Power cuts of more than 4 hours: In these cases, the temperature may have gone above 5 °C, and perishable foods such as meat, chicken, fish, eggs, milk or fresh cheese, or leftovers must be thrown away.

Whole fruit and vegetables can be kept at higher temperatures, so they can be eaten if they are still fresh, following the usual advice on washing them. Cut fruit and vegetables or prepared salads must be thrown away, just like perishable foods.

Some non-perishable foods, such as soft drinks, tinned food, alcoholic drinks or chocolate, are often kept in the fridge, although they do not need it. These products can stay in perfect condition out of the cold and be consumed without any problem.

## Recommendations for food in the freezer

- Freezers can maintain freezing temperature for at least 24-48 hours if the doors are not opened. Food can be refrozen if it has not thawed completely (it still has ice crystals on its surface, is hard and feels cold).
- In areas where the power cut may last more than 24 hours, it is important to check that the food has not thawed completely, in which case it can be refrozen.
