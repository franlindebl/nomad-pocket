What matters is keeping the people warm, not the whole house.

1. **Dress warmly indoors too.** Several thin layers keep you warmer than one thick one. Wear socks and slippers and keep a blanket handy for your legs.
2. **Live in one room**, the one that holds heat best, and close the doors of the others. If you can heat it safely, heat only that room. Put a rolled towel at the bottom of the door and close blinds and curtains.
3. **At night**, sleep in that same room if you can, with the window closed. Use extra covers, long underwear under your pyjamas and a hat. If you sleep on the floor, put blankets or mats underneath you too: the floor draws heat away.
4. **Eat and drink.** Have regular meals and drink fluids even if you aren't thirsty, above all water and hot drinks. Avoid alcohol: it makes you lose heat and feel the cold less.
5. **Move a little.** Don't sit still for more than an hour at a time; if walking is hard, stretch your arms and legs.
6. **Heat without danger.** Never use the oven, a gas hob, a barbecue, a camping stove or a generator to warm yourself indoors. If you use a brazier or a fireplace, air the room often. Turn off gas and electric heaters at night.

## Older people living alone

- They feel the cold less and can get hypothermia in their own home: a somewhat cool house, 15 to 18 °C, can be enough.
- Phone or visit them every day. Ask if they feel unwell and whether they need anything.
- If they can't get down to the street, bring up food and medicines for several days.
- If their home stays cold and there's no safe way to heat it, they should stay with family or friends, or in a heated place the authorities open.
- If you think someone has hypothermia, call 112.
