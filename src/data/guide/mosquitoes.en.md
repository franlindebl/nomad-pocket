1. **Get rid of standing water.** Once a week, empty and scrub, turn over, cover or throw out anything that holds water, indoors and outdoors: buckets, plant-pot saucers, vases, tyres, tarps, toys, pools, birdbaths and bins. That is where they lay their eggs.
2. **Know what they can spread.** After a flood, many mosquitoes hatch that bite but hardly ever spread viruses. The ones that can may increase from about 2 weeks on. In Spain, the common mosquito (which bites mostly at dusk and at night) can spread West Nile virus. The tiger mosquito (which bites by day) can spread dengue and chikungunya.
3. **Use repellent** on bare skin, with DEET, icaridin, IR3535 or oil of lemon eucalyptus. Follow the label: minimum age and how often to reapply. Used that way, it is also fine in pregnancy and breastfeeding. If you use sunscreen, sunscreen first and repellent after.
4. **For children**: no oil of lemon eucalyptus under 3 years. Don't put it on their hands, eyes, mouth or cuts; for the face, put it on your hands and spread it yourself.
5. **Cover up and keep them out**: long sleeves and long trousers, loose. Screens on windows and doors, nets over cots and pushchairs. Don't leave doors propped open.

## When to see a doctor

6. **Fever with headache**, body or joint aches, vomiting, diarrhoea or a rash after bites: see a doctor and say you were bitten by mosquitoes. For fever or pain, paracetamol: don't take ibuprofen or aspirin, because it could be dengue.
7. **A&E or 112** for high fever with a stiff neck, confusion, muscle weakness or shaking; or belly pain, 3 or more vomits in 24 hours, bleeding from the nose or gums, or blood in vomit or stools, even once the fever has gone down. Older people are more likely to become seriously ill.
