## Signs and symptoms

In general, a fracture can be suspected if the injured person shows several of the following local symptoms/signs (severe pain that increases on palpation, loss of function, swelling and bruising, more or less marked deformity and/or shortening of a limb, a wound with visible bone fragments) or general ones that reflect the presence of bleeding or the effects of the pain (tachycardia, pallor...)

## What to do

1. Avoid movements (one's own and the injured person's).
2. Examination:

- Primary assessment: vital signs.
- Secondary assessment, asking about sensations, pain, ability to move, comparing the limbs, shortening of them, deformities. etc.

3. Check the distal pulses (radial or pedal), to rule out internal bleeding.
4. In the case of an open fracture, apply sterile dressings to the wound.
5. Immobilisation
6. Cover the patient (Thermal protection)
7. Evacuation, keeping the vital signs under control and watching how the fracture is secured.

## Immobilisation

[…]

To immobilise a fracture, the following recommendations should be followed:

- Calm the injured person and explain beforehand any manoeuvre we have to carry out.
- Remove anything that could compress when the swelling sets in (rings, bracelets...)
- Immobilise with rigid material (splints) or with material that, once in place, does the same job as the rigid one (triangular bandages) (Fig. 4a and 4b).
- Pad improvised splints (pieces of wood, logs...).
- Immobilise one joint above and one below the point of the fracture.
- Immobilise in a functional position (if possible) and with the fingers or toes visible.
- Never reduce a fracture (do not put the bone back in place).
- Always evacuate to a hospital.

### Priority of action

- Avoid flexion-extension movements.
- Examine fully before acting.
- If the person has to be moved, always keep head, neck and body together as one block.
