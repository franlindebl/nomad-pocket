1. **Decide whether to go or stay.** In a city-wide blackout, keep travel to a minimum. If home is far, it may be safer to stay at work or with someone nearby until transport is back.
2. **Let home know** with a short text: where you are, what you are going to do and which way you will go. Make only short calls and call 112 only for an emergency. Trust only official information.
3. **Don't use lifts** while the blackout lasts: take the stairs.

## If you walk

- If the metro and trains are stopped, don't wait at the station for them to come back.
- Go with colleagues heading towards your area.
- Plan the route before you leave, along streets you know, and leave in time to arrive before dark: without street lights the streets are pitch black.
- Take water, comfortable shoes and a torch. Don't use your phone as a torch: it uses a lot of battery.
- Cross at pedestrian crossings and only when you see the cars have stopped.
- Keep away from fallen power lines.

## If you drive

With the traffic lights out:

1. Obey traffic officers first.
2. If there are no officers, follow the signs on the posts (stop, give way).
3. If there is no sign, give way to vehicles coming from your right. On a roundabout, whoever is already on it has priority.
4. Let pedestrians on pedestrian crossings go first.
5. Drive slowly and enter each junction with care, ready to brake: other drivers may not know who goes first.
