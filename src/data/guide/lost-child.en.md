## If you can't find your child

1. **On the beach or near water, check the water first** and tell the lifeguard straight away.
2. **Raise the alarm now, don't wait:** tell the staff there (lifeguards, security, shelter staff) or the police, and call 112. The sooner it is reported, the sooner the search starts.
3. **Describe the child:** name, age, height, features (glasses, braces), the clothes they are wearing, and when and where you last saw them. Show a photo if you have one.
4. **Stay calm and stay with the people searching.** If there are several of you, have another adult stay at your spot in case the child comes back.
5. **If you find the child yourself, say so at once** to whoever is searching, so they can call off the search.

## At a shelter

6. When you arrive, register the whole family at reception and agree with the children on a meeting point inside the shelter.
7. If a child is missing, tell the shelter staff at that very moment, and the police or 112.
8. 116 000 is the missing children line (Fundación ANAR): it guides and supports the family and passes the case to the police. It does not replace 112.

## So they don't get lost

9. Put a wristband or a note in their pocket with their name and your phone number. Some beaches hand them out at the lifeguard post.
10. Take a photo of them that day: it shows what they are wearing.
11. When you arrive, agree on a meeting point that is easy to recognise and show them where the lifeguard is.
12. Teach them what to do if they get lost: don't go off looking for you, stay where they are and ask a lifeguard, a police officer or a member of staff for help. Make sure they know their full name and yours and, if they are old enough, your phone number.
