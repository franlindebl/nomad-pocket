## Not too tight

Caution must be taken when splinting to ensure that it is not wrapped too tightly. If there is numbness, tingling or increased pain, or if the fingers or toes turn pale or bluish, loosen the bandage.

## Arm splint

- Splinting the fracture in the position found. DO NOT attempt to reposition or straighten the injury.
- Place rigid objects so that one is on each side of the injured arm or forearm.
- Positioning the rigid objects so that the joint above the fracture and the joint below the fracture will be immobilized.
- Apply padding between the arm and the splint.
- Secure the splint with cravats, gauze bandages, strips of cloth, or other securing materials.

Splints may be improvised from such items such as boards, poles, sticks, tree limbs, or cardboard. If nothing is available for a splint, the chest wall can be used to immobilize a fractured arm, and the uninjured leg can be used to immobilize (to some extent) the fractured leg.

Narrow materials such as wire, or cord should not be used to secure a splint in place.

## Sling

If a bandage is not available, a sling can be improvised by using the tail of a coat, shirt, or pieces of cloth torn from such items as clothing and blankets. Remember that the casualty’s hand should be higher than their elbow, and their fingers should be always showing.

## Check the splint

RECHECK the circulation after application of the splint. Check the skin color and temperature. This is to ensure that the bandages holding the splint in place have not been tied too tightly. A fingertip check can be made by inserting the tip of the finger between the bandaged knot and the skin.

## Tie the sling

The sling is secured by bringing the ends of the sling up and tie them at the side (or hollow) of the neck on the uninjured side (figure 13-7). The end or corner of the sling is then twisted and tucked at the elbow (figure 13-8).

Seek medical assistance.
