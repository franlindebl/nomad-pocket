## Bottles, teats and cups

1. **Wash your hands** with soap and water, or with hand sanitiser that has at least 60 % alcohol.
2. **Throw away leftover** milk or food. Take all the parts apart and put them in a clean basin you use only for this, never in the sink.
3. **Wash with safe water** (bottled, boiled or disinfected) and washing-up liquid, with a brush. Squeeze the soapy water through the holes of the teats and the valves. Rinse with safe water.
4. **Sterilise at least once a day** if the baby is under 2 months old, was born premature, is ill or has a weak immune system. Choose one way:
   - **Boiling:** cover the parts with safe water in a pot and boil them for 5 minutes. Take them out carefully, with clean tongs.
   - **With bleach, if you can't boil:** 1 teaspoon (about 5 ml) of unscented bleach for every 8 cups of safe water (about 2 litres). Put everything under, with no air bubbles inside, and leave it for 2 minutes.
   - Afterwards **don't rinse it**: any bleach left breaks down as it dries and won't hurt the baby.
5. **Let it air-dry** on unused kitchen paper, never with a tea towel. Store it dry and covered.
6. **If you have no safe water or soap** to wash with, use disposable cups. Throw away teats and dummies that have touched floodwater.

With no power you can boil on a gas hob; a camping stove or a barbecue only outdoors.

## Scissors (for example, to cut the cord)

7. **Boil them for 20 minutes** in a pot, together with the laces or tapes to tie the cord. Pour the water off and don't touch them until you use them. Pick them up by the handle, without touching the blades.
8. **If you can't boil:** wash them with soap and water and leave them in alcohol until you use them.

## Towels and cloths

9. Use clean, dry towels. Pick up bloodstained ones with gloves and wash them in cold water with bleach.
