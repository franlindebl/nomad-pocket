## Keep a bag ready

- Your pregnancy notes and medical reports, your ID and health card.
- Your medicines for at least 7–10 days and your pregnancy vitamins.

Ask your health centre where you would give birth if your hospital closed.

## In case you give birth before you get there

In a waterproof bag: gloves, a shower curtain or plastic sheet and absorbent pads for the bed, clean sheets, towels, blankets and a hat for the baby, large maternity pads, clean laces to tie the cord, sharp scissors, a bowl or bag for the placenta, nappies and soap. Choose a warm room.

## If you have to evacuate

1. Leave early; if you can choose, go somewhere near a hospital.
2. As soon as you can, tell the shelter, or whoever runs the evacuation, that you are pregnant and about any health problems.
3. In the car, always wear the seat belt: the lap belt below your bump, across your hips; the shoulder belt between your breasts. Never over your bump or under your arm.
4. Stop often to stretch, drink and eat something.
5. If labour is going very fast and you feel the baby coming, don't carry on: stop somewhere safe and call 112.

## Fever and medicines

- A fever of 38 °C or more in pregnancy is a warning sign: get medical care straight away and say you are pregnant.
- For fever or pain you can take paracetamol: the lowest dose that works, for the shortest time, as the leaflet says.
- Don't take ibuprofen or aspirin on your own: their leaflets advise against them in pregnancy and say they must not be taken in the last 3 months. If your doctor has prescribed aspirin, follow their instructions.

## If you are scared

Most women don't go into labour during an emergency, and most of those who do get to a hospital. Fast labours usually go well for mother and baby. If you can't get there, 112 will guide you by phone. Ask someone you trust to stay with you. Rest, drink water and tell someone how you feel; if the distress is too much, tell a health professional.
