Regular contractions every 5 minutes or more often: it's time to call or go to the maternity unit. Nobody can know exactly how long is left.

## How long it can take

- The early phase (up to about 4 cm) can last hours or days.
- After that, with a first baby, dilation usually takes about 8 hours, rarely more than 18. If you have given birth before, about 5, sometimes more than 12.
- Then comes the pushing stage: up to about 3 hours with a first baby and about 2 with later ones.
- When to leave also depends on previous births and the distance to the maternity unit: talk it over with your midwife.

## Signs that it is starting

- Contractions that get ever longer, stronger and closer together. Practice ones (Braxton Hicks) are irregular and don't build up.
- The mucus plug ("show"): sticky, pinkish mucus.
- Backache.
- Waters breaking: a trickle or a gush of clear fluid.

## Call or go now, even at night, if

- your waters break, especially if the fluid is greenish or brown, smells bad or has blood in it,
- you are bleeding more than the little blood in the show,
- the baby is moving less than usual,
- you are less than 37 weeks pregnant,
- a contraction lasts longer than 2 minutes or you have 6 or more every 10 minutes,
- you have a fever (over 37.5 °C) or shivers.

**Call 112** if you have a strong urge to push or feel the baby is coming.

**Also call 112** if, after your waters break, you see or feel the cord, a foot or a bottom in the vagina, or the baby is coming and the head is not first. Get on your hands and knees with your head down and your bottom up.

## Meanwhile, at home

- During the day, move about and walk; at night, rest or sleep if you can.
- Drink fluids and have a light snack if you feel like it.
- Slow breathing, a warm shower and a back rub help.
- Paracetamol as the leaflet says, if you need it.
- If your waters have broken, use a pad, not a tampon, to see the colour of the fluid.
- If the hospital is far away or there is no transport, have a clean place, dry towels, a blanket and a hat for the baby ready.
