Stop, sit down and look at the ankle before you decide whether to keep walking.

## It may be broken if

- You can't take 4 steps on the foot, not even limping.
- It hurts when you press the bone that sticks out on either side of the ankle (especially at the back or the tip), the bony bump on the outer edge of the foot halfway along, or the inner side of the midfoot.
- The ankle or foot looks deformed.
- The foot turns cold, pale or numb.

If any of these happens, don't try to walk out.

## If you can't put weight on it or it may be broken

1. Don't move the foot more than you have to. Immobilise it as it is.
2. Keep warm and insulated from the ground while you wait: in the cold, staying still chills you fast.
3. Call 112 and say where you are: 112 alerts the mountain rescue teams.

## If you can put weight on it and the pain is bearable

It is most likely a sprain.

1. Leave the boot on and lace it firmly so it supports the ankle. Take it off or loosen it if the foot turns numb, cold or pale; if it doesn't recover soon, stop and call 112.
2. With no boot, support the ankle with an elastic bandage, not so tight that it cuts off the circulation.
3. Move only if staying put is more dangerous (cold, nightfall, storm). Support the ankle firmly, lean on a stick or poles and go slowly. Otherwise stay and call 112.
4. If the pain or swelling gets much worse, or you can no longer put weight on it, stop and call 112.

Once you're back, carry on with ice, compression and elevation, and see a doctor if it isn't better in a few days.
