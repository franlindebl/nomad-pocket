## Chemical in the eye

1. **Rinse at once** with plenty of running water, **for at least 20 minutes**, without stopping.
2. Keep the eyelids open and take out contact lenses if you can do it easily.
3. Call **112** or the poisons information service (Información Toxicológica) while you rinse.

## Smoke, ash or dust

1. **Get out of the smoke**: go indoors or move away from it.
2. **Don't rub your eyes.** Wash your hands before touching them.
3. If you wear **contact lenses**, take them out and wear glasses until the eye is better.
4. **Rinse** them with clean water (tap or bottled, not hot) or saline, with a gentle flow.

## A speck, sand or an eyelash

5. Blink several times: tears often wash it out.
6. If it doesn't come out, rinse the eye with clean water or saline. In someone else's eye, look for it in good light without pressing on the eye: gently pull the lower lid down while they look up.
7. Don't use tweezers, cotton buds or anything else on the eye.
8. If it doesn't come out, or the pain, redness or feeling of something in the eye goes on, see a doctor.

## Something stuck in the eye, or a cut to the eye

9. **Don't remove it**, don't touch it and don't press on the eye. Don't put anything directly on the eye.
10. Cover it with something rigid that doesn't touch it, such as the bottom of a paper or plastic cup, taped from the forehead to the cheek.
11. Also cover the other eye, loosely, so the eyes move less. If the person has to walk or see to stay safe, leave the good eye uncovered.
12. **Call 112.**

## A blow to the eye

13. Gently hold a clean, cold cloth on it, **without pressing**: it reduces swelling. Have a doctor check it as soon as possible.

## Go to the emergency department (or call 112) if

- vision changes (blurred, double, loss of sight),
- there is blood inside the eye, or blood or pus coming out,
- there is severe pain, a headache, nausea or discomfort in light,
- they can't move the eye or keep it open,
- something hit it at high speed or went into it,
- a chemical got in: see "Chemical in the eye", above, and rinse at once.

If mild irritation isn't better after 24 hours, see your doctor or health centre.
