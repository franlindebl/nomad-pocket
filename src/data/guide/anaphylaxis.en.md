Anaphylactic shock is a severe and life-threatening allergic reaction that significantly impacts the circulatory system.

[…]

The treatment of anaphylactic shock is urgent and time sensitive. Immediate actions include calling for medical help and, if available, using the casualty’s epinephrine auto-injector. Anaphylactic reactions can manifest within seconds or minutes after exposure to an allergen, affecting the skin, respiratory, and circulatory systems.

[…]

### Assess Symptoms

- Observe for sudden and severe symptoms like difficulty breathing, wheezing, or shortness of breath.
- Check for a rapid or weak pulse.
- Look for swelling of the face, lips, or tongue.
- Note any hives or widespread skin redness.
- Be alert to signs of confusion, dizziness, or loss of consciousness.

### Look for Other Symptoms

- Be aware of nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, or a feeling of impending doom.

If anaphylactic shock is suspected, immediate medical assistance is crucial. The primary treatment involves administering epinephrine (adrenaline). If an epinephrine auto-injector is available, assist the affected individual in using it, or administer it yourself if you are allowed and trained. Dosage guidelines are typically provided with the injector—0.15 milligrams (mg) for children and 0.3 mg for adults weighing more than 66 pounds [≈ 30 kg, Nomad note].

Further doses of epinephrine may be necessary, administered every 5 to 10 minutes if prescribed and symptoms persist. Additionally, provide supportive measures for treating shock, respiratory failure, circulatory collapse, or cardiac arrest:

- Perform rescue breathing and external chest compressions if required.
- Monitor vital signs every three to five minutes until the casualty is stable.

[…]

Anaphylaxis is life-threatening, and immediate action is crucial. Seek professional medical help promptly and provide supportive care while awaiting arrival of medical personnel.
