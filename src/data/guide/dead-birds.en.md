If several birds die suddenly with no clear cause (backyard hens, ducks, pigeons, seagulls, storks…), it may be bird flu. People rarely catch it, but you need to report it and protect yourself.

1. **Don't touch them with bare hands**, nor their feathers, droppings or coop litter.
2. **Keep children, dogs and cats away.** Don't let pets sniff or bite the bodies.
3. **Report it straight away.** If they are your own birds, to your region's official veterinary services (in many regions, the local agricultural office, "oficina comarcal agraria"): bird flu is a notifiable disease. If they are wild birds, or you don't know whom to call, call 112. Say where they are, how many there are and what species.
4. **Don't bury or throw them away yourself** until you are told what to do: they may want to test them.
5. **Keep the rest of your birds shut in**, away from wild birds, and don't take birds or eggs off the premises until the official vet has spoken to you.
6. **If you are told to remove them yourself:** gloves, a mask (FFP2 if you have one) and, if you can, goggles. Put each bird in a rubbish bag without touching it, close it and throw it away where you are told.
7. **When you finish, wash your hands well** with soap and water. Don't touch your eyes, nose or mouth in the meantime. Clean your footwear and tools, and throw the gloves and mask away in a closed bag.
8. **Don't eat birds that were sick or were found dead.** Always cook poultry and eggs thoroughly.

## Watch your health for 10 days

If in the next 10 days you have a fever or chills, red or watery eyes, a cough, a sore throat, a runny nose, a headache or muscle aches, diarrhoea or shortness of breath:

- Call your health centre and say you have been in contact with sick or dead birds.
- Stay at home, away from other people, until you are told otherwise.
- If you find it hard to breathe, call 112.
