## Improvised nappies

1. **Fold several layers of absorbent cotton cloth** (a towel, a T-shirt or an old sheet) and put it between the baby's legs like a nappy.
2. **Fasten it loosely**, with a knot or sticky tape on the outside of the cloth, without squeezing the waist or thighs.
3. **If you add a waterproof layer on the outside** (plastic pants, a bag), keep it loose and off the skin, as it stops the skin breathing; never when the bottom is red.
4. **Change the cloth as soon as it is wet or dirty.**

## Washing cloth nappies

5. Tip the poo into the toilet or the poo bucket without touching anything else, and keep dirty nappies in a bucket with a lid until you wash them.
6. Wash them with water and soap or detergent, with no fabric softener. Rinse well, 2 or 3 times if the baby has or has had a sore bottom. Dry them completely.
7. Wash your hands with soap and water after every change (or use hand sanitiser) and wash the baby's hands too. Change and wash nappies away from food.

## Red or raw bottom, without creams

8. **Change the nappy very often**, as soon as it is wet or dirty.
9. **Clean with water only** and a soft cloth or cotton wool, without rubbing, or by squirting a little water. No soap, no wipes with alcohol or perfume, no talc, no antiseptic creams.
10. **Pat dry** or let it air-dry.
11. **Leave the nappy off for as long as you can**, with the baby lying on a towel.
12. If you can get to a pharmacy, ask for a barrier cream (zinc oxide or petroleum jelly) and put a thin layer on clean, dry skin.

## See a doctor or paediatrician

- If it isn't better in 2 or 3 days, or gets worse.
- If it spreads beyond the nappy (tummy, back, arms, face).
- If there are blisters, spots with pus or open sores.
- If the baby has a fever or seems very uncomfortable.
- If the baby is under 6 weeks old.
