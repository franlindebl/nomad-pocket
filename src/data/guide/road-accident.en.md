First **protect** (yourself and others), then **alert** 112, and only then **help**. If you don't know first aid, don't attempt manoeuvres: calling and staying with them already helps.

## Protect

1. Stop your car somewhere safe, if you can before the accident and off the carriageway. Put the handbrake and hazard lights on and switch off the engine.
2. Switch on the **V-16 beacon** and place it from inside the car on the highest point (the roof). Since 1 January 2026 it is the only valid signal: don't walk along the road to put out triangles.
3. Put on the **hi-vis vest** before getting out, and get out on the side away from traffic.
4. Don't walk or stay in the lanes or on the hard shoulder: wait somewhere safe off the road, for example behind the crash barrier. If there's no way to get out safely, stay inside with your seat belt on.
5. In the crashed cars, switch off the ignition if you can. Nobody should smoke nearby. Don't take anyone out of an unstable vehicle.

## Alert

6. Call **112** and say: road and kilometre point (or street and town), direction of travel, how many vehicles and what kind, how many injured and how they are, and whether people are trapped, there is a fire, a lorry carrying dangerous goods or a car in the water. Leave your phone number.

## Help

7. **Don't move the injured** or take them out of the car, unless there is a risk of fire or explosion. If they must be moved, move them as one block: head, neck and body together.
8. **Don't take off a motorcyclist's helmet**, unless they are not breathing normally and you must start CPR: then remove it, ideally with a second person supporting the head and neck.
9. If they are bleeding heavily, press hard on the wound with a clean cloth.
10. Loosen tight clothing and protect them from cold or heat.
11. **Don't give them anything to eat or drink.**
12. If they don't respond but are breathing normally, leave them as they are and keep checking they are still breathing. If they don't respond and aren't breathing normally, tell 112 and start CPR.
13. Stay with the injured until the emergency services arrive.
