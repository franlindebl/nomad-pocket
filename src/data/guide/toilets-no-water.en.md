1. **If only the water is off and the drains are fine**, you can keep using the toilet: pour a bucket of water straight into the bowl to flush it. Keep drinking water for drinking. If you have no water to pour, don't use the normal toilet: make a bucket toilet.
2. **After an earthquake, don't flush** even if water is running: the waste pipes may be broken. Close the toilet off so nobody uses it and make a bucket toilet.
3. **Make a bucket toilet:** two sturdy buckets with lids, about 15–20 litres each, one for pee and one for poo. Keeping them apart smells less and is safer.
4. **Poo bucket:** line it with a sturdy bin bag. After each use, add a handful of dry material to cover it (sawdust, soil, dry leaves, cat litter or shredded newspaper) and close the lid. Toilet paper goes in this bucket.
5. **Pee bucket:** put 2–3 cm of water in the bottom and no paper. Empty it every day, mixed with more water, onto an unused patch of ground or a green space.
6. **As a last resort**, put a sturdy bag inside the toilet bowl, covering the whole bowl, with dry material inside. Then tie it and put it in an outdoor rubbish bin.
7. **Wash your hands** with soap and water after each use, or use hand sanitiser.
8. **Empty the poo bucket** at least every 3 days, with gloves if you have them. Tie the bag, pushing the air out, and leave it in a bin with a lid, apart from other rubbish, or bury it in a hole about 50 cm deep. Follow what the health authorities say. Wash the bucket and disinfect it with diluted bleach if needed.
9. **Away from water and food:** always empty or bury far from wells, rivers, streams and vegetable gardens, and keep the toilet away from where you prepare food.
10. **If someone has vomiting or diarrhoea**, they use a separate bucket; empty and disinfect it with extra care.
11. Pads, tampons, nappies and wet wipes go in the rubbish, not in the buckets.
