## In a power cut or without internet

1. **Listen to the radio.** A battery or wind-up radio works, or the car radio. Don't run the engine in a garage or any closed space: car exhaust contains carbon monoxide.
2. **Tune in to Radio Nacional de España** (RNE, the public broadcaster) **or a local station.** By law, in an emergency the media must broadcast the authorities' warnings free of charge.
3. **Write down on paper, before you need them,** the frequencies of RNE and of a local station.
4. **Trust only official sources:** 112, Protección Civil, your town council or your regional government, AEMET. Don't forward unconfirmed messages.

## Which radio to have

- A radio that runs on batteries; better if it also has a crank.
- Spare batteries, kept with the emergency kit.

## Sirens

Near some chemical plants, dams and nuclear power stations there are sirens, and ES-Alert does not replace them. The civil protection authorities announce each test beforehand.

An example, the chemical risk areas of the Basque Country:

- **Wavering sound** (three one-minute tones separated by five-second silences): alert. Go into a building, stay in an inside room, close doors and windows, seal air inlets, switch off the air conditioning and ventilation, and listen to the radio.
- **Steady, flat sound for 30 seconds:** end of the alert. Don't go out before you hear it or the authorities announce the end.

If you hear a siren and don't know what it means, check your phone (ES-Alert), listen to the radio and follow their instructions.
