1. **Don't split your pills, stretch them or skip doses** on your own to make them last. Get more before they run out.
2. **Some are dangerous to stop suddenly:** antiepileptics, insulin, corticosteroids (such as prednisone), heart and blood-pressure tablets (such as bisoprolol), anticoagulants (such as Sintrom) and medicines for anxiety, sleep or depression. If you run out of one of these, get help that same day.

## Where to get it

3. **A pharmacy, including the duty pharmacy (farmacia de guardia).** With your health card (tarjeta sanitaria), any pharmacy can give you what is on your e-prescription, also in another region of Spain. The list of duty pharmacies is on pharmacy doors.
4. **Explain your situation to the pharmacist**, even without a prescription. Bring the box, the medical report or the treatment sheet: they will tell you what can be done.
5. **Health centre (centro de salud):** ask for an appointment, also by phone or through your regional health service's app. The doctor can renew your e-prescription or write you a paper one. Ask about a sick note (baja) too if you need one.
6. **If the e-prescription system is down, or you left it behind while travelling or evacuating:** bring your health card, the box or a photo, the treatment sheet or the name of the active ingredient and the dose. If the pharmacy can't give it to you, go to the health centre or A&E (urgencias).

## Insulin

7. In Spain insulin needs a prescription. If you come from another country, go to a health centre or A&E. A prescription from a doctor in another EU country is valid in Spanish pharmacies, though the brand may have a different name.
8. Without insulin, blood sugar rises: first great thirst and passing a lot of urine. **Call 112** if fast deep breathing, fruity-smelling breath, vomiting that stops you drinking, stomach pain or great tiredness appear.

## Call 112 if

- a seizure lasts more than 5 minutes;
- there is chest pain or shortness of breath;
- there is a sudden, very bad headache, a drooping mouth, weakness on one side or trouble speaking.

Always carry a list of your medicines (name, dose and times), on paper and on your phone.
