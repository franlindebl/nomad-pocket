## Indoors

- Stay inside with the doors and windows closed. Close the fresh air intake if your cooling or ventilation system has one (sometimes called “recirculate mode”).
- Create a “cleaner air room” in your home where you can keep levels of smoke as low as possible. Use a portable air cleaner in the room.
- Seek shelter elsewhere if you cannot tolerate smoke and cannot stay cool indoors with the windows closed.
- Do not burn candles or incense or use gas, propane, wood-burning stoves, fireplaces, or aerosol sprays. Avoid cooking on a stove or in an oven, especially when using high heat or for a long cooking time.

## If you must go out

If you must be outside in smoky conditions, an N95 respirator reduces your exposure to the fine particles in smoke if it fits snugly to your face and does not leave gaps. Children under 2 years old should not wear a respirator.

## Your health

- If you have asthma, follow your asthma management plan.
- If you feel sick, reduce your exposure to smoke and contact your health care provider.

## Children

- Think about evacuating if your child has trouble breathing or other symptoms that do not get better.
- If your child has severe trouble breathing, is very sleepy, or will not eat or drink, reduce their exposure to smoke and get medical help right away.

## Cleaning up ash

People with heart or lung disease, older adults, and pregnant women should not participate in ash cleanup efforts. Children should not be near ash or sites contaminated with ash.

- Wear work gloves, long-sleeved shirts, long pants, socks, and sturdy, thick-soled shoes to avoid skin contact.
- Wear goggles and head coverings to keep ash out of your eyes and hair.
- Avoid dry sweeping or dusting. Never use a leaf blower. Before sweeping indoor and outdoor hard surfaces, mist them with water to keep dust down.
- Ash should be stored in plastic bags or other containers to prevent it from being stirred up.
