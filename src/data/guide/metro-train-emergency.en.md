1. **Stay calm and listen to the staff** and the announcements. Do what they tell you.
2. **Raise the alert:** use the carriage intercom or the emergency button to talk to the driver. If someone is unwell, call 112.
3. **If there is smoke:** cover your nose and mouth with a handkerchief or a piece of clothing, keep low (the clean air is near the floor) and move away from the smoke into another carriage, if they are connected and you can pass. If you are on a platform or in a station, leave by the nearest exit, away from the smoke.
4. **Do not open the doors on your own or get down onto the tracks.** There is a live rail or overhead cable, and other trains may come. This is the greatest danger.
5. **If the doors do not open**, do not force them. Call on the intercom: staff will open them or tell you how. Do not open the doors yourself unless staff tell you to, or there is immediate danger, such as a fire at the platform.
6. **Wait for an organised evacuation.** If you have to leave into the tunnel, staff will guide you; follow the evacuation lights and signs away from the smoke, without running or pushing.
7. **If the train stops in open country**, stay inside. Ask the staff and wait for their instructions. Getting down onto the tracks is dangerous even if hours go by.
8. **In a power cut in the tunnel:** be patient, use your phone's light sparingly, help children, older people and people with reduced mobility, and do not crowd towards the doors.
