1. **If you are not sure they have died, or the death was sudden, call 112.** They will tell you what to do, including whether to start CPR.
2. **If the death was expected** (a long illness), call their family doctor or their health centre; out of hours, 112. A doctor has to see them and sign the medical death certificate: without it the death can't be registered and they can't be buried.
3. **If it was violent, from an accident, or there is any doubt**, call 112 and don't move the body or touch anything: the police or Guardia Civil and the court take over.
4. **While you wait**, cover them with a sheet. Keep the room as cool as you can: turn off the heating, lower the blinds and shut the door.
5. **Hygiene.** If you touch them, wear gloves if you have them and wash your hands with soap and water afterwards, and before eating. After a natural disaster, dead bodies don't usually cause epidemics. If they had an infectious illness, touch them as little as possible and say so when you call.
6. **If nobody comes** (a disaster, no phone), keep trying 112 and tell the police, Guardia Civil, firefighters or a health centre in person: the authorities have to deal with it. Don't bury or move them yourself. Write down their name and the date and time of death, and leave their ID card with the body.
7. **The paperwork.** With the medical certificate, call a funeral home: it usually makes the declaration at the Civil Registry, which registers the death and issues the licence for burial or cremation. The family can also declare it.
