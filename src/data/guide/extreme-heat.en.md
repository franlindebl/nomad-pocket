## General recommendations to prevent the effects of high temperatures on health

1. Drink water and fluids often, even if you are not thirsty and regardless of the physical activity you do.
2. Avoid drinks with caffeine, alcohol or a lot of sugar, as they can encourage dehydration.
3. Although anyone can have a heat-related problem, pay special attention to: babies and children, breastfeeding and pregnant women, as well as older people or people with illnesses that may get worse with the heat (such as heart disease, kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, obesity, cancer, conditions that limit mobility, dementia and other mental illnesses, as well as drug or alcohol abuse).
4. Stay as long as possible in cool, shaded or air-conditioned places, and cool yourself down whenever you need to.
5. Try to reduce physical activity and avoid doing sports outdoors in the middle hours of the day.
6. Wear light, loose clothing that breathes.
7. Never leave anyone in a parked, closed vehicle (especially minors, older people or people with chronic illnesses).
8. See your health professional about symptoms that last more than an hour and that may be related to the high temperatures.
9. Keep your medicines in a cool place; heat can change their composition and their effects.
10. Eat light meals that help replace the salts lost through sweat (salads, fruit, vegetables, juices, etc.).
