1. **There is no right way to feel.** It is normal to feel shock and numbness, deep sadness and crying, anger and guilt, including guilt about what was said or not said. These feelings can come back suddenly, even when you thought they had passed.
2. **The pain becomes less intense over time**, but there is no timetable. Waves can return on anniversaries or when something reminds you of the person.
3. **Talk about how you feel** with a friend, a family member, a professional, or a group of people who have been through something similar.
4. **Look after the basics:** do some exercise, eat regular meals and don't try to do everything at once; set small targets. Avoid alcohol, cigarettes, gambling or drugs to relieve the pain.
5. **If you couldn't say goodbye:** funerals are a way of saying goodbye and sharing memories. If you weren't there, you can talk about the person and say their name, write down what you remember and gather photos or keepsakes in a box.
6. **With children:** be honest and use simple words. Say "died": "lost" or "passed away" confuse.
7. **Ask for help at your health centre** if your mood has been low for more than 2 weeks, if what you are doing is not helping, or if months go by and you cannot return to your usual activities.
8. **If you are thinking of ending your life or of not going on living:** in Spain, call 024 (free, confidential, 24 hours). To talk to someone, Teléfono de la Esperanza, 717 003 717. In immediate danger, 112.
