Bleeding or spotting in the first weeks is common and is not always a miscarriage: often the pregnancy carries on. Only an examination, with a scan, can tell what is happening.

## Call 112 if you are bleeding and also

- you have severe pain in your tummy (the pain stops you doing anything),
- the tip of your shoulder hurts,
- you feel dizzy, faint, sick or look very pale,
- the bleeding is heavy: you soak a pad soon after putting it on.

Sudden, very severe pain in the tummy, often low and on one side, with shoulder pain or dizziness, can be an ectopic pregnancy (outside the womb) that has burst. It is internal bleeding and life-threatening: it needs surgery as soon as possible. It can happen even if you haven't had a positive test yet.

## While help is coming

1. Lie down. If you feel dizzy, raise your legs.
2. Use pads, not tampons: that way you can see how much you are bleeding.
3. Keep your pregnancy notes at hand to take with you.

## If you are only spotting, with no pain or mild pain

- Get seen today: call your health centre or go to the hospital emergency department.
- Pain low on one side of the tummy, even if mild, also needs checking straight away.
- If any of the signs above appear, call 112.
- A miscarriage that has started can't be stopped, and bed rest doesn't change the outcome.
- For pain, paracetamol as the leaflet says.

## Afterwards

- Bleeding can last up to 2 weeks and pain a few days.
- It is normal to feel sadness, anxiety or grief like losing a loved one, for the partner too. Talk to whoever is with you and ask for psychological support if you need it.
