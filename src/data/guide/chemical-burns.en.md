## Chemical burns

The handling of these injuries will be based, first of all, on removing the chemical from the injured person's skin.

Steps to take:

[…]

- We must take special care with splashes that may reach us or with direct contact of our skin with the chemical substance.
- During the shower, all objects in direct contact with the skin must be removed: glasses, clothing, shoes, rings, bracelets, watches and other jewellery.

There are chemicals that react on contact with water, producing more heat. Even so, in these cases too we will apply a CONTINUOUS WATER SHOWER as the treatment, because the possible initial reaction would be neutralised by the large amount of water.

[…]

## Eyes

In the case of eye burns, the eyes must be irrigated, keeping them open, for at least 20 minutes. These injured people will be evacuated while that irrigation continues, using rubber bulb syringes of water or irrigation bottles.

## Conclusion

[…]

The correct course of action is as follows:

1. ELIMINATE THE CAUSE.
2. COOL WITH WATER.
3. COVER OR COVER UP THE INJURED AREA.
4. TAKE THE INJURED PERSON TO A MEDICAL CENTRE.
